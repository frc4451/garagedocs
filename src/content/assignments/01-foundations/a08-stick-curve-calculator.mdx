### Scenario

A joystick that reads `0.04` when nobody is touching it will creep your robot across the
field. A joystick that maps linearly to motor output is twitchy at low speed and gives the
driver no fine control. Both problems are fixed by putting the raw reading through three
small transformations before it reaches a motor.

This assignment is really about **decomposition**: each transformation is a method you can
reason about and test on its own.

### What to build

A single file `StickCurve.java` with these `public static` methods:

<ContentTable
  headers={["Method", "What it does"]}
  rows={[
    ["<code>double deadband(double raw, double threshold)</code>", "Returns <code>0.0</code> when <code>Math.abs(raw)</code> is below <code>threshold</code>. Otherwise returns <code>raw</code> unchanged."],
    ["<code>double signPreservingPow(double value, double exponent)</code>", "Raises the magnitude to the exponent and puts the original sign back. So -0.5 squared is -0.25, not +0.25."],
    ["<code>double clamp(double value, double min, double max)</code>", "Holds <code>value</code> inside the range."],
    ["<code>double applyCurve(double raw, double deadbandThreshold, double exponent)</code>", "Calls the other three in order: deadband, then the curve, then clamp to -1.0 through 1.0."]
  ]}

### Requirements

- Each method does exactly one thing and returns a value. None of them print
- `applyCurve` calls the other three rather than repeating their logic
- Use `Math.abs` and `Math.pow` rather than hand-rolling either
