### Scenario

A trailing moving average replaces each reading with the mean of recent readings. It reduces short-term variation but delays the response to a change.

At the beginning of the array, average only the readings available. Do not fill the missing positions with zeros.

### What to build

A single file `SmoothOne.java`:

1. Read `n`, a positive `int`, then `n` doubles into a `double[] readings`
2. Read `window`, an `int` from 1 to `n` inclusive
3. Build `double[] smoothed` where `smoothed[i]` is the mean of `readings` from index
   `max(0, i - window + 1)` through index `i`, inclusive at both ends
4. Print the index, the raw value and the smoothed value, each to two decimals

```text
i   raw     smoothed
0   10.10   10.10
1   12.20   11.15
2   11.80   11.37
3    9.40   11.13
4   10.00   10.40
5    8.00    9.13
```

### Requirements

- The smoothing works on any window from 1 to `n`, for every valid input, beyond the sample
- The first entries average fewer than `window` values; they must not read past the start of
  the array
- Print both columns to two decimals

### Stretch goals

- Make each output O(1) instead of O(window): keep a running sum, add the reading that
  enters the window and subtract the one that leaves. The whole smoother becomes O(n),
  and the difference is the whole point of [Big-O](/java/fundamentals/java-big-o#the-hidden-loop). Check it still
  matches your two-loop version exactly, including the ragged start.
- Add a centred moving average that looks both ways, and describe what it costs you on a
  live robot. The answer involves the word *latency*.
- Report the standard deviation of the raw readings alongside the smoothed trace, so you can
  say how noisy the encoder was.
