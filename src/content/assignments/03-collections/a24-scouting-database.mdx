### Scenario

In [A21](/java/assignments/a21-scouting-csv) you moved scouting rows around in arrays. Arrays
are the right tool when you know the size up front. They are the wrong one the moment
entries arrive over the course of a weekend, get filtered, get ranked, and get deleted
because someone typed the wrong team number.

`ArrayList<T>` is the everyday list. This assignment is the small database you will
use at a competition. You will also make one design decision that separates a class
you can trust from one you cannot.

### What to build

**`TeamEntry`**: a record holding `teamNumber`, `teamName`, `drivetrain`, `avgPoints`
and `climbsHigh`, with a compact constructor that validates: team number 1 to 99999,
name not blank, `drivetrain` one of `TANK` / `MECANUM` / `SWERVE` / `OTHER`, `avgPoints`
not negative. `pickScore()` is `avgPoints`, plus 25 when the team climbs high.

**`ScoutingDatabase`**: one `ArrayList<TeamEntry>` field and nothing else:

### The one that matters

`all()` is the decision worth arguing about. Returning the internal list directly lets
any caller add to your database or clear it without going through a single line of
your validation. `Collections.unmodifiableList(entries)` returns a wrapper that reads
straight through to your data and throws on any attempt to write.

It is a *view*, not a copy: later additions through `add` show up in a view somebody
already holds. That is usually what you want, and it is worth knowing that it is what
you are getting.

### Stretch goals

- Add `top(int k)` returning the k best by pick score. Write it once with `sorted` +
  `limit` and once by sorting and taking a sublist, then decide which you would rather
  read at 11pm in a pit.
- Wire this to `CsvIO` from [A21](/java/assignments/a21-scouting-csv): `load(String)` and
  `save(String)` commands that round-trip the whole database to disk.
- Give `filter` a sibling that takes two predicates and ands them. Then notice that
  `Predicate` already has `and`, and delete it again.
