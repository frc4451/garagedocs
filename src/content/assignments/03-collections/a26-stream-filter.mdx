### Scenario

You have a season of scouting entries and a list of questions:

- Which teams run swerve?
- Who are the top five by pick score?
- What is the average climb rate for each drivetrain?
- Is there anyone here who averages over 80?
- Who is the first team matching *this* condition?

Every query is read-only. You are not changing the list, you are producing an
answer from it. That is the shape streams are for.

You will write each query **twice**, once as a stream and once as a loop. This is not
busywork: the point is that after ten of these you will have an opinion about which
reads better, and it will be your own rather than borrowed.

### What to build

One file, plus the `TeamEntry` record. `pickScore()` is `avgPoints` plus 25 times
`climbRate`.

### Stretch goals

- Add `summary(List<TeamEntry>)` returning a record `Summary(int count, double
  totalScore, double maxClimbRate, String topTeamName)` from a single pipeline. Look at
  `Collectors.teeing`. Start with three passes, then decide whether the
  one-pass version is clearer.
- Take one query, write it a third time with `parallelStream()`, and time all three over
  100,000 entries with `System.nanoTime`. Be ready for the parallel version to lose.
- Rewrite `topKByPickScore` so it does not sort the whole list. A bounded priority queue
  gets you O(n log k) instead of O(n log n), but the result is harder to read, which
  is the trade to notice.
