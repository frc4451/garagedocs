**Topic:** recognising algorithmic complexity at sight, and checking it empirically.
**Cites:** [Big-O and Reading Complexity](/java/java-big-o), [Searching](/java/java-algo-searching), [Sets and Maps](/java/java-ds-sets-maps).
**Difficulty:** medium. **Estimated time:** 60–90 minutes.

### Scenario

This is a reading assignment before it is a writing one. Ten short snippets; tag each
with its complexity and say why in one sentence.

The skill being built is narrow and valuable: looking at five lines and *immediately*
knowing whether they are O(1), O(n) or O(n²). That instinct is what stops you from
putting a 50 ms algorithm inside a loop that runs every 20 ms — which is a bug that
looks like "the robot feels laggy" and never like a stack trace.

### Part 1 — tag the snippets

For each of the ten methods in the starter, write a comment above it giving:

1. The complexity in terms of `n`
2. One sentence on why
3. Whether best, average and worst differ

The snippets are already written; the `// TAG:` lines are where your answers go. Nothing
in the editor grades these — that is the point. Getting them right is between you and
the timing table in Part 2.

### Part 2 — make one tag pay off

Snippet `j` is the one with a fix. `List.contains` is O(n) and it runs once per element,
so the whole thing is quadratic. Rewrite it:

- **`jFast(int[])`** — the same distinct values in the same first-occurrence order, with
  a `HashSet` doing the membership test. O(1) average per check makes the whole method
  linear.
- **`median(long[])`** — the median of the samples, the mean of the middle two when the
  count is even, **without reordering the caller's array**. Timing noise is the entire
  reason you take a median rather than one reading.

`main` then times three snippets at three sizes using your `median`, and prints the
table.

### Stretch goals

- Time `j` against `jFast` at the same three sizes and watch the gap widen. That is the
  assignment's whole argument in one table — one tag, one rewrite, a different curve.
- Add a snippet of your own that mixes two shapes the ten do not cover: two sequential
  O(n) loops (still O(n) — the reason why is worth writing down), or an O(n) loop around
  an O(log n) recursion.
- Take `h` and memoize it, then time `h(35)` before and after. Then work out what the
  complexity became, and why it is not obvious from the code any more.
