## Scenario

Label ten snippets with their growth in work as the input grows. Explain the loops, searches, or recursive calls supporting each label.

Then measure several input sizes. Timings can illustrate growth, but warmup, garbage collection, and system load mean a few measurements cannot prove a Big-O bound.

## Part 1: Tag The Snippets

For each of the ten methods in the starter, write a comment above it giving:

1. The complexity in terms of `n`
2. One sentence on why
3. Whether best, average and worst differ

The snippets are already written; the `// TAG:` lines are where your answers go. Nothing
in the editor grades these. You must assess your own reasoning. Compare your reasoning with the linked Big-O lesson and discuss uncertain tags with your mentor. The timing table measures only three snippets and cannot validate all ten tags.

State which case your label describes. For snippets whose work can end early, give
the best and worst cases separately. For hash-based work, distinguish expected or
average O(1) membership from a worst-case guarantee. Assume valid inputs for the
snippets as written: `a` receives a non-empty array, `g` receives an ascending sorted
array, and `h` receives a non-negative `n`. Use positive `n` for `f` and `i`. Analyze `i` for values below the point where doubling its `int` counter can overflow; do not use huge inputs to test its mathematical growth. For array methods, `n` means the array length.

## Part 2: Make One Tag Pay Off

Snippet `j` is the one with a fix. `List.contains` is O(n) in the worst case and it
runs once per element, so the whole method has quadratic worst-case growth, reached
when many values are distinct and the growing result list must be scanned. Rewrite it:

- **`jFast(int[])`**: the same distinct values in the same first-occurrence order, with
  a `HashSet` doing the membership test. O(1) average per check makes the whole method
  linear on average. A hash table does not promise constant-time checks in every
  possible worst case.
- **`median(long[])`**: the median of the samples, the mean of the middle two when the
  count is even, **without reordering the caller's array**. The median gives one middle value from repeated measurements and reduces the influence of an unusually high or low reading.

`median` receives at least one non-negative timing sample. Because it returns `long`,
an even sample count uses integer division for the mean of the two middle values. The
provided samples stay far below overflow.

`main` then times three snippets at three sizes using your `median`, and prints the
table. Treat that table as an observation from one machine and one run. The ratios
may be zero, unstable, or unlike the predicted curve at these small sizes because the
timer resolution, just-in-time compilation, garbage collection, and other processes
can dominate the work. Derive Big-O from the code, not from a benchmark result.
