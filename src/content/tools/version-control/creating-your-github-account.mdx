### What the account is for

Your personal GitHub account identifies you when you work on repositories. The account belongs to you. A team organization can give that account access to shared repositories without knowing your password or owning your account.

You need a verified email address before you can create repositories and use several other GitHub features. Use an address you expect to keep. A school address may stop working after graduation, but you can add it as a second address later if your school or team needs it.

### Create the account

<StepsBox title="Sign up for GitHub">

1. Open [GitHub's signup page](https://github.com/signup).
2. Create a free personal account. Use a strong, unique password and save it in your password manager.
3. Choose a username you are comfortable showing on repository pages, pull requests, and your public profile.
4. Complete GitHub's signup prompts.
5. Open the verification message GitHub sends to your email address and follow its link.
6. Sign in again, open your profile, and copy your exact username. You will give the username to your mentor when you need team access.

</StepsBox>

Do not create a shared account for the whole Automation Team. Separate accounts keep commits, reviews, and permissions connected to the person who performed each action.

### Protect the account

Open your profile menu, select **Settings**, then select **Password and authentication**. Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) and follow GitHub's setup instructions.

Save the recovery codes when GitHub displays them. Store them somewhere secure that you can reach if you lose your phone or computer. Do not keep the only copy on the device used for authentication.

<RulesBox title="Keep account credentials private">

Never send anyone your password, authentication code, recovery code, personal access token, or private SSH key. A mentor needs your GitHub **username** to invite you, not your credentials.

</RulesBox>

### Join the team organization

1. Send your exact GitHub username to the mentor or organization owner responsible for access.
2. Watch your email and GitHub notifications for the organization invitation.
3. Open the invitation while signed in to the correct account and accept it.
4. Confirm that the organization appears under your profile menu and that you can open the repository your mentor names.

An invitation can expire. If it no longer works, ask the organization owner to send it again. Some organizations require 2FA before you can accept an invitation or open their private repositories.

### Connect commits to your account

Your GitHub username and your Git commit identity are related, but they are configured separately. The next lesson shows how to set the name and email stored in local commits.

Use an email address connected to your GitHub account so GitHub can attribute pushed commits to you. If you do not want a personal address stored in commits, GitHub provides a private `noreply` address in **Settings → Emails**.

### Before you continue

- You can sign in to your own GitHub account.
- Your email address is verified.
- 2FA is enabled and your recovery codes are stored safely.
- You know your exact username.
- If your mentor sent an organization invitation, you accepted it and can open the assigned repository.
