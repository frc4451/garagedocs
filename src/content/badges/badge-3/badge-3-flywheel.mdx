The student characterizes the flywheel, controls its measured speed, and validates the tuning on a
separate run. Start after the calculus, output-capability and roller work in Steps 2–4. Complete the
physical fixture preparation before hardware characterization. Read [Feedforward](/frc/feedforward),
[Tunable Constants and Characterization](/frc/tunable-constants-and-characterization),
[Manual Tuning: Flywheel](/frc/manual-tuning-flywheel), and [FuelSim](/frc/fuelsim).

## Characterize Before Refining Feedback

Adapt Cobra's `SingleRoller.quasistaticCharacterization` into a bounded command owned by the parent
subsystem. A quasistatic test slowly increases effort so acceleration contributes less to the
measured response. A slow ramp still needs checking; it does not guarantee a steady-state
measurement.

Capture configuration when the command starts. Define a maximum effort, maximum duration, speed
bound, and cancellation action before running it. On every exit, command zero effort and restore the
saved configuration in unconditional cleanup. Cobra's referenced routine is a starting point to
inspect: its restoration follows a continuous command, so copying that sequence does not guarantee
restoration after cancellation.

Collect timestamp, requested and measured effort, speed, direction, and output mode. Exclude
stationary samples from a moving-region fit and inspect acceleration and saturation. Fit voltage or
current against speed using consistent units, require at least two distinct speeds, and reject a
zero or near-zero regression denominator. A fitted slope from nearly identical speeds is not useful
evidence. Save the samples and the method used to select them.

Validate the resulting gain choice on a separate run, then tune feedback against the agreed speed
error and settling targets. Compare Talon FX voltage and torque-current control and Talon FXS
voltage control. Keep the gain units beside every value. SysId is encouraged exploration; it does
not replace the required Cobra adaptation.

## Connect Measured Speed to a Shot

The saved speed response becomes the input to
[Step 6: FuelSim Launch Experiment](/badges/badge-3-fuelsim-experiment). That module owns the launch
model, scoring shot, controlled miss and model assumptions. Finish the speed-control evidence here
before changing launch parameters.

## Independent Change

The mentor selects a different flywheel goal or simulated inertia. The student predicts spin-up,
settling and steady speed, repeats from the same initial conditions, and explains the measured
response. Keep the controller configuration, fitted data and independent validation run for the
launch experiment.
