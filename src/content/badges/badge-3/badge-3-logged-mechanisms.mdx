Badge 2 established numeric AdvantageKit logs, IO contracts, physics simulation and 2D
measured-versus-goal drawings for all four mechanism families. In Badge 3, the student applies that
drawing pattern to Cobra's intake, verifies it against saved logs and replay, and then animates
the intake CAD from the same measurements. Reuse the Badge 2 fundamentals instead of treating them
as new material. Start after the [Step 7 pivot work](/badges/badge-3-pivot) and finish before
[Step 9: Subsystem-Owned States](/badges/badge-3-states).

## Review the Three Objects

Badge 2 used these objects for all four mechanism families. Review
[What a Mechanism Drawing Shows](/frc/what-a-mechanism-drawing-shows) and
[Ligaments and Components](/frc/ligaments-and-components) before applying them to Cobra.

| Object | Job | Student Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `LoggedMechanism2d` | Owns the drawing canvas. | Choose a size that contains the motion. |
| `LoggedMechanismRoot2d` | Anchors a drawing at a position on that canvas. | Identify the pivot or fixed base. |
| `LoggedMechanismLigament2d` | Draws a segment attached to a root or another segment. | Identify its length and its angle relative to its parent. |

Give the drawing's lengths and root positions as typed `Distance` values, as the Badge 2 constants
do, and convert to meters at the drawing API. A root-mounted ligament at 0 degrees
points right; positive rotation turns toward up. Child angles are relative to their parent, so
attaching a segment to an already rotated segment changes its displayed direction. Start with a
single fixed root for the intake pivot. Moving roots and full robot drawings are extensions after
this example works.

The drawing only displays data. Physics stays in the IO implementation, control stays in the
existing component and subsystem, and the physical reference comes from the Step 7 procedure. Keep
RobotContainer responsible for construction and bindings.

## Draw the Measured Pivot and Its Goal

Start with two distinguishable ligaments attached to the same root: a thin measured arm and a wider
goal arm. Give them different names and colors. Use an `Angle` for each value until the drawing API
requires a numeric angle.

Start from your Badge 2 `CobraIntakeMechanism` from
[Badge 2 Step 9](/badges/badge-2-mechanism-composition#build-the-composed-2d-mechanisms). That
visualizer composed the Badge 2 `PivotMechanism` helper and recorded its own output as
`Intake/Pivot/Mechanism2d`. In Badge 3, Cobra's `Intake` subsystem owns the drawing and records it
as `Intake/Mechanism2d`. Read
[Badge 2: Keep the Visualizer Beside Its Parent](/frc/mechanisms-per-subsystem#badge-2-keep-the-visualizer-beside-its-parent)
and [Naming the Key](/frc/mechanisms-per-subsystem#naming-the-key), then explain in your notes what
moved: the log key, and which object records it.

Then read what Cobra already has. In the fork, `IntakeConstants` defines `rootMechanismConfig` for a
canvas and root, and `measuredPivotMechanismConfig`, `setpointPivotMechanismConfig`, and
`goalPivotMechanismConfig` for Cobra's `PivotMechanism` helper, each a 12 in arm. Nothing builds or
logs them yet: `Intake` logs only whether the pivot is near its goal. Cobra's helper wraps a single
ligament whose `update(Angle)` sets its angle, and it has no method to hide a ligament. The three
arm configs share a width of 10 and use three similar greens, so change them to meet the thin
measured, wide goal, different-color requirement, and add a way to hide the goal arm. Starting from
`rootMechanismConfig` for the canvas is one option.

Choose a canvas that contains the motion. The arm lengths are schematic display dimensions, not
physics parameters. The schematic uses 0 degrees to the right and 90 degrees up. The separate robot
CAD view has its own mounting transform; these two views need not face the same direction on screen.

The subsystem owns the drawing and updates it immediately after its normal input-processing call. In
this illustrative snippet from `Intake.periodic()`, `mechanismDrawing` is the student's drawing
object, and its method names are placeholders for your own: Badge 2's helper, for example, exposes
its canvas as `getMechanism()`. Cobra's `Pivot.getGoalPosition()` returns a plain `Angle` even when
no goal is active, so first give the pivot a `getGoal()` that returns `Optional<Angle>`, as Badge
2's `PivotMechanism.update` expects:

```java
this.endeffector.periodic();
this.mechanismDrawing.update(this.endeffector.getPosition(), this.endeffector.getGoal());
Logger.recordOutput("Intake/Mechanism2d", this.mechanismDrawing.getDrawing());
```

This is part of the existing `periodic()` method. Do not add a second call to the mechanism's
`periodic()` to draw it. Publish the drawing every loop after changing it. Drawing updates run
on the robot thread even when the selected vendor simulation advances its physics on a Notifier.

Drive the measured arm from the processed IO measurement in REAL, SIM, and REPLAY. Drive the goal
arm from the requested goal, and hide it when no goal exists. A stopped pivot can still have a
measured position. Do not substitute a goal or the simulator's private state for that measurement.

Decide whether `getGoal()` should be empty after the robot disables, and write down why. Then read
`SingleRoller.stop()` and `SingleRoller.periodic()` in the fork and predict what Cobra's pivot
reports as its goal after a disable. Make your migrated pivot match the decision you wrote.
If feedback is invalid, retain the corresponding validity/status trace and explain that the last
displayed position is not proof of current physical position.

Read [Measured Against Goal](/frc/measured-against-goal). Distinguish the final requested goal from
a profile's intermediate reference. A profile-reference arm is an extension when that signal is
available and correctly labeled; do not label the final goal as the profile reference.

## Verify the Drawing Before Trusting It

1. Feed known measured angles of 0, 20, and 90 degrees into the drawing while the goal stays at
   60 degrees. Confirm the measured arm changes and the goal arm does not follow it.
2. Use 0.25 rotations and confirm the measured drawing matches 90 degrees. Record the expected
   orientation and units before running the check.
3. Remove the goal and verify its line is hidden while the measured arm remains available.
4. Run a simulated pivot move. Compare the drawing with the numeric position and goal traces at
   the same timestamp. Inspect a point during motion where the two values differ.
5. Disable or interrupt the mechanism and compare its numeric state and drawing. The drawing must
   follow measured feedback; it must not animate a successful arrival solely because a command
   requested it.

Write a test that checks the drawing's serialized angles and goal visibility. The test supports the
exercise; the student also saves a log and demonstrates the timeline comparison.

## Review in AdvantageScope

Review [The Mechanism Tab](/frc/simulation-in-advantagescope#the-mechanism-tab) and
[The Line Graph](/frc/simulation-in-advantagescope#the-line-graph-commanded-versus-measured). Open
the saved WPILOG in AdvantageScope, add a Mechanism tab, and drag `RealOutputs/Intake/Mechanism2d`
into its control pane. Use the same timeline timestamp in a line graph of the pivot's position and
goal. Save the log, a screenshot with that timestamp, and enough layout instructions for another
student to reproduce the view.

For an AdvantageKit replay, follow [Replaying a Match](/frc/log-replay#replaying-a-match). The
Starter's `Robot` reads the selected log and writes the replay beside it with a `_sim` suffix, as
`<log>_sim.wpilog`. Compare the regenerated `ReplayOutputs/Intake/Mechanism2d` in that file with the
original output and recorded inputs. Explain the difference between playing back a log in
AdvantageScope and rerunning robot code with replayed inputs. The drawing requires no new physics or
hardware in REPLAY.

## Import and Animate the Intake CAD

After the 2D checks, complete the bounded Week 6 exercise in
[Visualizing Cobra](/frc/visualizing-cobra) with the Week 6 Cobra AdvantageScope asset,
`Robot_Cobra2026Week6`. The mentor supplies its `advantagescope-assets` folder with the
Starter; copy it into the root of the fork and follow its README. Export the intake separately,
record units, align its origin and hinge, and animate the measured intake pose. Keep the hood static
for this exercise. Compare known lengths and the 0, 20, and 90 degree poses before interpreting a
saved log.

Cobra logs no component poses, so add one. Read
[Component Poses](/frc/mechanisms-in-3d#component-poses) first, and
[Mounts and Moving Roots](/frc/mounts-and-moving-roots) if you later move the root. Log the
component pose array, for example as `Intake/ComponentPoses`, and attach it in AdvantageScope's 3D
field view. The asset README orders the array as `[intake, hood]`: the intake entry comes from the
measured angle, and the hood entry is an identity pose that keeps the static hood where it was
exported. Log fuel positions from the Step 6 fuel world beside it. Record the asset version,
component order, transforms, and log with B3-08.2, and include the reproduction steps in the B3-15.1
handoff. The 2D drawing and 3D CAD articulation are separate evidence, and neither replaces the
physical reference and repeatability checks in B3-07.2–B3-07.4.

S means simulation, W means written reasoning, and M means mentor-observed review. Save an evidence
link, student explanation, mentor signature, and date for every row.

| B3-08.2 | Measurement and goal checks | S: Verify 0, 20, and 90 degrees, equivalent units, a distinct fixed goal, and absent-goal visibility. Compare a moving and disabled/interrupted run with the numeric inputs at matching timestamps. Repeat the known-pose checks on the animated intake CAD. |

## For the Mentor

`Badge3MentorSolution` contains a complete small example: `IntakeMechanismDrawing`, with a 39.4 in
(1 m) canvas and a 15.7 in (0.4 m) arm, and `IntakeMechanismDrawingTest`, which checks the
serialized drawing angles and goal visibility. Its `Intake` logs `Intake/Mechanism2d` and
`Intake/ComponentPoses`. Its Badge 3 library moves the drawing helper to a `visualization` package
(Badge 2's is `visualizations`) and changes its API: Badge 2's `PivotMechanism` owns a canvas and
exposes `update(Angle, Optional<Angle>)` and `getMechanism()`, while the Badge 3 helper wraps one
ligament built from a `Config` and exposes `getLigament()`, `setVisible(boolean)`, and
`update(Angle)`, so the drawing class owns the canvas and root. The student copy of the CAD asset,
with a student README, is in the solutions repository at `badge_03/starters/advantagescope-assets`;
the mentor solution keeps its own copy, which its `IntakeVisualizationTest` and render script use.
