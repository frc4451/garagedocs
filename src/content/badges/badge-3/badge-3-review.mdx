The student uses evidence to diagnose behavior, leads a code review, helps another member learn, and proposes a concrete lesson improvement. Plan these activities at badge entry and collect evidence alongside the technical modules once suitable work is available. Step 12 reviews that evidence and completes any missing demonstration. Read [Reading a Match Log](/frc/reading-a-match-log), [Robot Network Troubleshooting](/frc/robot-network-troubleshooting), [Log Replay](/frc/log-replay), and [Pull Requests](/tools/pull-requests).

## Diagnose Before Changing Code

Record the software version, configuration, symptom, and time interval. Compare requested behavior with measurements, outputs, state transitions, and console messages. Use Driver Station logs and robot logs together where available. A voltage drop, packet loss, or loop overrun is an observation to investigate, not a complete explanation by itself.

For each prepared case below, the student names at least two plausible causes, chooses a confirming check, and states what result would change the diagnosis. The mentor supplies a recorded trace or marks the table as a synthetic exercise. Do not invent a claim that a physical fault was reproduced.

| Prepared Observation | Reasoning Task |
| --- | --- |
| Requested speed is steady; measured speed falls while applied voltage saturates. | Separate model/load limits, power limits, and feedback choices. Identify the current and battery signals needed next. |
| Driver Station reports lost communication near a pause in robot data. | Compare timestamps, packet information and robot restart indicators before blaming the control loop. |
| A periodic loop exceeds its time budget after enabling a new feature. | Compare timing before and after; inspect blocking work, camera processing and repeated configuration. |
| A path ends while the robot is short of its goal. | Distinguish timeout from arrival and compare reference, estimate and available simulation truth. |
| The robot has a held piece but no launch occurs. | Trace requested state, reference validity, readiness and feeder output. |

Then investigate one of the student's own saved runs. Write the evidence, hypothesis, confirming test, result, and remaining uncertainty. Use replay for a new diagnostic when the relevant inputs were recorded.

## Lead a Code Review

Choose a peer's change, a prepared change, or a Badge 2 candidate's work. Begin by asking the author what behavior changed and how it was checked. Trace one request through ownership, units, coordinate frames, IO, logging, and cleanup. Check invalid inputs, timeout, cancellation and disable where relevant.

Record a substantive question, the author's explanation, the evidence inspected, and any follow-up. Finding a defect is not required. A useful review can confirm a sound design and explain why the tests cover it. The student follows up on agreed revisions instead of treating a comment count as completion.

## Teach and Improve a Lesson

Help a Badge 1 or 2 student understand a task. Ask the learner to predict an outcome, let them operate the tools, and use their explanation to choose the next hint. The supervising mentor observes the contribution and judges readiness; there is no hours quota or required fault scenario.

For lesson feedback, identify an existing page, the point where learning became difficult, and a proposed improvement. Include the original location and enough replacement explanation, example, or ordering to make the proposal reviewable. Discuss it with the mentor. Publication, a pull request and a reader trial are optional.

## Badge Check

S means simulation, H means hardware, W means written reasoning, and M means mentor-observed review. The student records an evidence link, mentor signature, and date for each row. The mentor asks the student to explain a change and its result before signing.

| Row | Work | Done When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B3-11.1 | Technical diagnosis | W/M: Interpret prepared robot/Driver Station logs and the student's own runs, distinguish electrical, network, timing and control hypotheses, and justify the next confirming check. |
| B3-11.2 | Student-led code review | M: Lead a substantive review of a peer or prepared change, ask questions, analyze ownership and behavior, and follow up on revisions. Finding a defect is not required. A Badge 2 candidate's work may be used. |
| B3-11.3 | Teaching and contribution | M: Explain Badge 1 concepts to a candidate and help Badge 1 or 2 students succeed. The Automation Team mentor judges observed contribution; there is no hours quota or required technical fault scenario. |
| B3-11.4 | Lesson feedback | W/M: Identify an existing lesson, a concrete learning difficulty and a suggested improvement; discuss with a mentor. Publication, a PR and reader trial are optional. |
