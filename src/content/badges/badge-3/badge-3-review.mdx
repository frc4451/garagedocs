The student uses evidence to diagnose behavior, leads a code review, helps another member learn, and
proposes a concrete lesson improvement. Plan these activities at badge entry and collect evidence
alongside the technical modules once suitable work is available. Step 14 reviews that evidence and
completes any missing demonstration. Read [Reading a Match Log](/frc/reading-a-match-log),
[Robot Network Troubleshooting](/frc/robot-network-troubleshooting), [Log Replay](/frc/log-replay),
[Controllers and the Console](/tools/advantagescope-controllers-and-the-console) for opening Driver
Station `.dslog` and `.dsevents` files, the loop-overrun symptoms in
[Running the Simulator](/frc/running-the-simulator), and
[Reviewing Pull Requests](/tools/pull-requests#reviewing-pull-requests).

## Diagnose Before Changing Code

Record the software version, configuration, symptom, and time interval. Compare requested behavior
with measurements, outputs, state transitions, and console messages. Use Driver Station logs and
robot logs together where available. A voltage drop, packet loss, or loop overrun is an observation
to investigate, not a complete explanation by itself.

For each prepared case below, the student names at least two plausible causes, chooses a confirming
check, and states what result would change the diagnosis. The mentor supplies a recorded trace or
marks the case as a synthetic exercise, and the student's write-up keeps that label.

| Prepared Observation | Reasoning Task |
| --- | --- |
| Requested speed is steady; measured speed falls while applied voltage saturates. | Separate model/load limits, power limits, and feedback choices. Identify the current and battery signals needed next. |
| Driver Station reports lost communication near a pause in robot data. | Compare timestamps, packet information and robot restart indicators before blaming the control loop. |
| A periodic loop exceeds its time budget after enabling a new feature. | Compare timing before and after; inspect blocking work, camera processing and repeated configuration. |
| A path ends while the robot is short of its goal. | Distinguish timeout from arrival and compare reference, estimate and available simulation truth. |
| The robot has a held piece but no launch occurs. | Trace requested state, reference validity, readiness and feeder output. |

Then investigate one of the student's own saved runs. Write the evidence, hypothesis, confirming
test, result, and remaining uncertainty. Use replay for a new diagnostic when the relevant inputs
were recorded.

## Lead a Code Review

Choose a peer's change, a prepared change, or a Badge 2 candidate's work. Begin by asking the author
what behavior changed and how it was checked. Trace one request through ownership, units, coordinate
frames, IO, logging, and cleanup. Check invalid inputs, timeout, cancellation and disable where
relevant.

Record a substantive question, the author's explanation, the evidence inspected, and any follow-up.
Finding a defect is not required. A useful review can confirm a sound design and explain why the
tests cover it. The student follows up on agreed revisions until they are complete.

## Teach and Improve a Lesson

Explain a Badge 1 concept to a candidate, and help a Badge 1 or 2 student with a task from
[Badge 1](/badges/badge-1) or [Badge 2](/badges/badge-2). Ask the learner to predict an outcome, let
them operate the tools, and use their explanation to choose the next hint. The supervising mentor
observes the contribution and judges readiness; there is no hours quota or required fault scenario.

For lesson feedback, identify an existing page, the point where learning became difficult, and a
proposed improvement. Include the original location and enough replacement explanation, example, or
ordering to make the proposal reviewable. Discuss it with the mentor. Publication, a pull request
and a reader trial are optional; GarageDocs accepts issues and pull requests at
[frc4451/garagedocs](https://github.com/frc4451/garagedocs).

## Badge Check

S means simulation, H means hardware, W means written reasoning, and M means mentor-observed review.
The student records an evidence link, an explanation in the student's own words, the mentor's
signature, and the date for each row. The mentor asks the
student to explain a change and its result before signing.

| Row | Work | Done When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B3-14.1 | Technical diagnosis | W/M: Interpret prepared robot/Driver Station logs and the student's own runs, distinguish electrical, network, timing and control hypotheses, and justify the next confirming check. |
| B3-14.2 | Student-led code review | M: Lead a substantive review of a peer or prepared change, ask questions, analyze ownership and behavior, and follow up on revisions. Finding a defect is not required. A Badge 2 candidate's work may be used. |
| B3-14.3 | Teaching and contribution | M: Explain Badge 1 concepts to a candidate and help Badge 1 or 2 students succeed. The Automation Team mentor judges observed contribution; there is no hours quota or required technical fault scenario. |
| B3-14.4 | Lesson feedback | W/M: Identify an existing lesson, a concrete learning difficulty and a suggested improvement; discuss with a mentor. Publication, a PR and reader trial are optional. |

## For the Mentor

`Badge3MentorSolution`'s test run writes saved logs that fit two prepared cases:
`NEVER_READY.wpilog` in `build/evidence` (a held piece with no launch) and
`COLLECTION_TIMEOUT.wpilog` (an attempt that ends without possession). The first three cases need a
recorded robot or Driver Station log from the fixture; otherwise mark them as synthetic exercises.
