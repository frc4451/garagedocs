Badge 3: Advanced FRC is the Automation Team capstone. The student refines mechanisms, integrates
sensing and autonomous behavior, explains test evidence, and helps another member succeed. The
mentor assesses technical ownership and readiness for leadership.

Every student receives the same preserved `Badge3Starter` baseline and completes the full migration
in an individual fork. The completed `Badge2MechanismReference` stays available for comparison.
Badge 2 or mentor-verified equivalent work is the prerequisite. Supplied code establishes the
starting point; the student explains it and demonstrates the additions below.

## Materials and Release Status

Each step starts the student from material they can open: the file in their fork of `Badge3Starter`
(Cobra as it competed, on GitHub as Cobra2026Private), the GarageDocs lesson that teaches the topic,
and the pinned vendor documentation. The mentor supplies the Starter, the completed
`Badge2MechanismReference`, and the Week 6 Cobra AdvantageScope asset.

The integrated Cobra collect-and-score outcome remains pending calibration and verified scoring.
The preserved Starter has its own limited verification procedure. Each student row closes with the
student's own evidence. Record the package revision and open validation items before beginning
instruction.

The required work includes the bounded intake CAD import/animation exercise in Step 8 and
heading-only observed-cluster assistance in Step 10. Both run in simulation and use those steps'
rows: the CAD exercise keeps the hood static, and cluster assistance turns only the heading.

Step 8 applies the Badge 2 logged 2D drawing pattern to Cobra's intake after the pivot work, then
animates the intake CAD from the same measurements before state integration. The schedule below
includes one two-hour meeting for the drawing and its evidence; confirm the actual time in the
student pilot.

The table gives the main learning sequence. Review the Badge 2 hardware evidence at entry; each
advanced physical demonstration follows its mechanism's simulation checks. Calculus and simulation
may continue while hardware appointments are pending. FuelSim and pivot work can proceed alongside
one another. Object-detection geometry can begin from the Badge 2 vision prerequisites. The Step 12
autonomous integration waits for the mechanisms, states, observations, FuelSim and the Step 11
combined simulation. Leadership evidence accumulates throughout.

Step 11 combines the simulators before the one-piece autonomous runs: maple-sim owns chassis
motion and contact, FuelSim owns fuel and scoring, and PhotonVision observes that same world.
Complete B3-11.1–B3-11.3 before the final Step 12 runs; the B3-11.4 reproduction by another member
can follow with the handoff in Step 15.

Plan approximately twelve to thirteen weeks with two two-hour meetings each week. Reserve Cobra
hardware appointments while simulation, written work, and peer review continue. This is about 50
supervised student hours; preparing and validating the teaching references is separate authoring
work. Treat the schedule as a planning estimate. Record active work, equipment waiting, mentor
assistance, and any home work separately. If the pilot runs long, revise the support and the
schedule, keep every required outcome, and record any home work openly.

## Evidence and Support

The October 13, 2026 revision numbered the steps 1–15 and renamed every row after its step. Use
the [curriculum revision record](/badges/badge-3-sign-off#curriculum-revision-record) to translate
an earlier identifier or packet. The site rows govern the revised path.

Every required row needs an evidence link, an explanation in the student's own words, a mentor
signature, and a date. Define performance targets before tuning and retain successful and failed
runs. A live visualization does not replace a saved log.

The first written check diagnoses gaps without blocking instruction. Corrections continue with the
lessons. After the assessed topics, complete an independent 100% mastery attempt, then a separate
independent 100% final. Worksheet reasoning is corrected to 100% with feedback. Completed practical
evidence remains credited. See [Written Checks](/badges/badge-3-written-checks).

Badge 2 introduces the motor controllers; Badge 3 provides Talon FX hardware for the advanced work.
The required physical checks are a verified voltage baseline, FX roller, flywheel and pivot work, a
voltage/current comparison, characterization, CANrange, pivot references, and stationary camera
checks. Matching Badge 2 FX, FXS or Nova evidence counts toward them. The FXS and Nova review rows
apply only to the controller the student used in Badge 2; other physical vendor ports are optional.
The advanced FX/FXS simulation comparisons are required. Autonomous collection and scoring stay in
simulation. Intentional physical jams, automatic retry, QuestNav, Choreo, SystemCore and SPARK MAX
instruction are outside the required path.

## Calculus for Controls

The existing [Calculus for Controls](/badges/badge-3-calculus) module keeps identifiers C1–C5 and
the current worksheet. Earlier completed work may count after mentor review. Its calculations become
evidence for B3-02.1.

## For the Mentor

Each step ends with a For the Mentor note naming the matching files in the mentor solution. Begin
with `TRAINING-MATERIALS.md` in `Badge3MentorSolution`. It identifies the preserved Starter, the
Badge 2 reference, the migrated Cobra, and the legacy `curriculum-reference` examples, and maps each
step to its reference file and test. Keep the projects' dependency versions and commands separate,
and keep the answer keys and worked examples outside student packages. `ROW-EVIDENCE.md`
distinguishes supplied demonstrations from student evidence, and `PILOT-RECORD.md` records the pilot
schedule. A successful build and a passing mentor test suite show that the references work; they do
not close student rows.
