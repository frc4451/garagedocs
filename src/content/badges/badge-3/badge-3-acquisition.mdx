The student integrates one simulated collection and scoring attempt using BLine, the subsystem-owned
states from Step 9, and camera observations. The task is simulation-only. Start after Steps 6, 9, 10
(including its delayed-observation check) and 11 (the [combined simulation](/badges/badge-3-maple)).
The student works in their fork of `Badge3Starter`.

Read these before starting:

- [BLine](/frc/bline)
- [Autonomous Simulation](/frc/autonomous-simulation)
- [FuelSim](/frc/fuelsim)
- [Subsystem State Machines](/frc/subsystem-state-machines)

## Start from Cobra's Autonomous

In the fork, `AutonomousManager` builds Cobra's routines from fixed BLine paths, intake deploy and
stow commands that wait until the pivot is near its goal, and timed shooting segments such as
`handleInterpolateShooterToHub` from Step 1. Each path command comes from `drive.getBLineBuilder()`,
the builder `Drive` configures in its constructor. `BLineManager` constructs a separate builder with
its own PID controllers, but nothing in the fork constructs `BLineManager`. No routine reads camera
observations, and nothing confirms possession: collection is assumed from the path and the clock.

`Drive`'s builder adds `.withDefaultShouldFlip()` and, in SIM only, `.withPoseReset(this::setPose)`.
Before reusing it for the approach, find what each option does in SIM, using
[Wiring It to the Drivetrain](/frc/bline#wiring-it-to-the-drivetrain) and BLine's FollowPath builder
options (in Sources). Explain why a path to an observed goal, already expressed in field coordinates
from the estimated pose, needs neither.

The one-piece sequence replaces that assumption with observed states. Write down, for each state,
the measurements that permit its transition. Route normal forward-feed requests through the
readiness policy from Step 9.

## Establish the Sequence

Build the sequence from the four mechanism families in the migrated library (roller, flywheel, pivot
and elevator), with commands owned by their parent subsystems.

Run the sequence in the Step 11 combined simulation; a single-piece stand-in is optional for unit
tests of the transition policy. Record the initial robot pose, stationary piece location, search
region, return pose, and scoring target, and start every comparison from those same conditions.

1. Travel to the search region, then wait there for a valid PhotonVision observation, moving on
   after a stated wait.
2. Select the target and compute the acquisition pose using the intake offset. Construct the BLine
   approach from the estimated robot pose. Do not reset odometry to a detected piece or to the true
   pose.
3. Approach and run the intake. Confirm collection through the possession IO. Reaching a path
   endpoint is not proof that collection occurred.
4. Return with the piece, prepare the flywheel and pivot, and wait in place for measured readiness,
   moving on after a stated wait.
5. Issue a single launch event, consume the held piece once, and record the resulting simulator
   score. A launch completes the action; a simulator score determines whether it succeeded.

Each path command requires the drive subsystem and runs until BLine reports the path finished; do
not put a time limit on a path. Distinguish arrival within the stated tolerance from a path that
finished outside it; command completion alone cannot authorize collection or scoring. Time limits
belong only on waits in place, such as waiting at the pickup for possession or a scoring window
while stationary, and a wait that times out moves the routine on instead of ending autonomous. In
this one-piece attempt, moving on ends the attempt in a defined final state: outputs stopped, the
reason logged, and nothing marked held or scored. In a multi-piece routine, the same timeout starts
the next path instead. On interruption, stop drive and feeder outputs and preserve a consistent
piece state.

Ordinary command composition of the BLine path is acceptable when each state transition still comes
from a measurement: for example, a sequence or deadline group that runs the follower and the intake
request, as in [Autonomous Routines](/frc/command-groups#autonomous-routines). A coordinator
subsystem that owns the sequence's states and runs the follower as a child command is also
acceptable.

Add the routine to the fork's existing `LoggedDashboardChooser` in
`RobotContainer.configureAutonomousRoutines`, beside Cobra's routines, so it can be selected from
the dashboard in SIM. If the student selects it another way, the README records how to select and
run it.

## Connect One Fuel World

One fuel world owns the piece: the field positions of its available pieces feed the camera world's
simulated targets, its intake event changes the possession IO, and its launch operation consumes the
held piece and adds the projectile. The robot state machine reads camera and possession inputs; it
does not query the fuel world's exact piece location to decide where to drive.

FuelSim does not synchronize PhotonVision targets automatically. Reuse the Step 10 adapter that
drives the camera world from the fuel world; it already replaced the ten static placeholder targets
that the fork's `Robot.simulationInit` adds. Remove collected targets so no duplicate piece stays
visible after acquisition. A missed projectile may return to the field according to the model; it is
not automatically held again. [Registering Intakes](/frc/fuelsim#registering-intakes),
[Launching Fuel](/frc/fuelsim#launching-fuel), and
[Reading the Result](/frc/fuelsim#reading-the-result) cover the FuelSim calls.

Launch from the measured flywheel speed and the launch elevation converted from the measured hood
position at launch. That converted elevation replaces the fixed launch elevation allowed in Step 6.
Record the launch elevation convention, flywheel radius, slip factor, release height, and
field-relative robot velocity. These are the declared assumptions for the scoring rows. Keep a
scored count distinct from held state and launch count. Mirror relevant world state into the saved
log so another member can reconstruct the event.

## Required Failure Runs

Build seven integrated runs: one successful run, one run for each of the first four failure cases
below, and two interruption runs, one during driving and one during scoring. Save each run as a
named WPILOG. Inspect state, refusal, camera and possession inputs, measured mechanism values,
estimated pose, inventory, launch count, and score together. A passing launch test does not
establish that the projectile scored or that the physical robot is tuned.

| Case | Injection | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No visible piece | Remove or hide the piece before the search. | The search wait times out after its stated wait; no possession or launch is invented. |
| Target lost | Stop observations before collection. | The stale-target rule is visible and the attempt stops or recovers as documented. |
| Collection timeout | Prevent possession confirmation at the attempted pickup. | The pickup wait times out and the attempt ends in a defined state; held and scored remain false. |
| Never ready | Keep measured speed or angle outside tolerance. | The feeder stays stopped and readiness times out. |
| Interruption | Cancel once during driving and once during scoring. | Drive and feeder stop; the piece is neither duplicated nor consumed twice. |

The student demonstrates each case through the integrated robot code and its actual IO path in the
combined simulation; policy tests against a stand-in do not replace these runs.
[Unit Tests in Simulation](/frc/unit-tests-in-simulation) shows how to run each case as a repeatable
test that saves its log.

## Keep Arrival and Visibility Checks Consistent

Give the follower a small translation tolerance to reach the intake pickup region; 3 in works in
simulation. Within a short distance and heading of the pickup goal, such as 6 in and 8 degrees,
acquisition can finish the short final approach on the estimated pose while the intake hides the
target.

Repeat the collection-timeout run and the successful run with added loop delay, for example 10 ms:
the pickup wait must still time out when possession never arrives, and one confirmed launch must
still occur when it does. Each failure test checks that the attempt ended with the logged reason for
its own case. The slower collection-timeout run must end with a collection timeout, not an early
target-lost reason; the target-lost case keeps its own run.

## Score under Declared Assumptions

B3-12.1 and B3-12.7 close when an integrated run in the combined simulation records a score under
the declared geometry and model assumptions; collection and a launch alone do not complete them. Use
[Step 6's hood and table conventions](/badges/badge-3-fuelsim-experiment#apply-cobras-hood-and-table-conventions)
to read the measured hood position and Cobra's preserved empirical tables. The declared assumptions
take their launch elevation from
[Work Out the Launch Elevation from the Hub Table](/badges/badge-3-fuelsim-experiment#work-out-the-launch-elevation-from-the-hub-table)
and their slip factor from
[Fit and Check the Slip Factor in FuelSim](/badges/badge-3-fuelsim-experiment#fit-and-check-the-slip-factor-in-fuelsim).
Do not change empirical table values solely to turn a simulation assertion green.

The Step 6 launch experiment and any test that drops fuel through a hub to check score counting
remain useful within their scope; neither establishes Cobra's shot calibration. Calibrating the shot
against physical launches is an optional extension: reconcile measured release speed, geometry, and
the preserved empirical tables, and preserve misses in the record. Set aside some shots for
validation and do not use them to fit the tables.

## Independent Change

The mentor changes the initial piece location within the camera's field of view and the Step 10
range limit. The student predicts the acquisition pose, runs the full sequence, and explains any
difference using observations, path error, possession, readiness, and score. The student saves the
changed-location run's log with the successful run and the failure logs.

## Badge Check

S means simulation and W means written reasoning. The student records an evidence link and an
explanation in the student's own words for each row in the fork's README. The mentor asks the
student to explain a change and its result, checks each row, then signs and dates the step on the
[sign-off sheet](/documents/badges/badge-3/badge-3-sign-off.pdf).

| Row | Work | Done When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B3-12.1 | Baseline one-piece sequence | S: Start at a specified pose, travel to a search region, detect through PhotonVision, approach using BLine, confirm collection through simulated inputs, return, wait for readiness, launch, and record a simulated score under declared assumptions. Log explicit available/held/scored state. |
| B3-12.2 | No visible piece | S: Search with no piece visible; the search wait times out after a stated time and the attempt ends in a defined stopped or recovery state without inventing possession. |
| B3-12.3 | Target lost | S: Lose the observation before collection; apply the documented stale-target rule and log the outcome. |
| B3-12.4 | Collection timeout | S: Reach the pickup without confirming possession; the wait at the pickup times out after a stated time and the routine moves on without marking a piece held or scored. |
| B3-12.5 | Never ready | S: Hold a mechanism outside readiness; the readiness wait times out after a stated time and the routine moves on without launching, or recovers if readiness returns. |
| B3-12.6 | Interruption | S: Interrupt during driving and during scoring; verify documented safe outputs and consistent piece state. |
| B3-12.7 | Score in the combined simulation | S/W: Reuse the Step 6 launch experiment with measured hood position. One fuel world owns the piece; reuse the Step 10 camera adapter, remove collected targets, and drive the possession IO from the same world. Consume one held piece once and use the simulator's scoring result. Recheck failure cases affected by the integration. |

## For the Mentor

`Badge3MentorSolution` maps its states in `frc/robot/subsystems/acquisition/Acquisition.java` to
their measurements in `ONE-PIECE.md`. Its approach paths are built in
`frc/robot/subsystems/drive/Drive.java`, and `FuelCameraTargets` supplies the camera world's
targets. The reference defines the routine in `frc/robot/autonomous/routines/OnePiece.java` and
lists it, in SIM only, in the `AutoSelector` routine chooser, which replaces the Starter's
`LoggedDashboardChooser`. `OnePieceScenario` runs the seven cases (`SUCCESS`, `NO_PIECE`,
`TARGET_LOST`, `COLLECTION_TIMEOUT`, `NEVER_READY`, `CANCEL_DRIVE`, and `CANCEL_FEED`) through
`OnePieceSimulationTest` and the per-case tests (`NoPieceSimulationTest`,
`TargetLostSimulationTest`, `CollectionTimeoutSimulationTest`, `NeverReadySimulationTest`,
`CancelDriveSimulationTest`, and `CancelFeedSimulationTest`), and saves their logs under
`build/evidence`; `SlowCollectionSimulationTest` and `SlowSuccessSimulationTest` repeat two of them
with added loop delay. `FeedPolicyTest` covers the feed policy alone. The reference's `SUCCESS` run
and its slow variant record one launch and one score under the fitted shot model from
`CobraHubTableShotTest`. `SHOT-CALIBRATION.md` holds the calibration worksheet.
