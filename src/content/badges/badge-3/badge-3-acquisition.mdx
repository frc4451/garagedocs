The student integrates one simulated collection and scoring attempt using BLine, the state-owned
subsystem, and camera observations. Start after Steps 9 and 10, including the delayed-observation
check, the required FuelSim experiment in Step 6, and the
[Step 11 maple-sim integration](/badges/badge-3-maple). Read [BLine](/frc/bline) and
[Autonomous Simulation](/frc/autonomous-simulation). The task is simulation-only.

## Establish the Sequence

Record the initial robot pose, stationary piece location, search region, return pose, and scoring
target. Start from the same conditions for every comparison. First prove the sequence with a
single-piece field model and sensor adapter, then connect the FuelSim world from Step 6.

1. Travel to the search region and wait for a valid PhotonVision observation within a stated time
   bound.
2. Select the target and compute the acquisition pose using the intake offset. Construct the BLine
   approach from the estimated robot pose. Do not reset odometry to a detected piece or to
   simulation truth.
3. Approach and run the intake. Confirm collection through the possession IO. Reaching a path
   endpoint is not proof that collection occurred.
4. Return with the piece, prepare the flywheel and pivot, and wait for measured readiness within a
   stated bound.
5. Issue a single launch event, consume the held piece once, and record the resulting simulator
   score. A launch completes the action; a simulator score determines whether it succeeded.

Give the path commands drive requirements and bounded execution. Distinguish successful arrival from
a timeout; command completion alone cannot authorize collection or scoring. On interruption, stop
drive and feeder outputs and preserve a consistent piece state.

## Connect One Field Model

One world owns the piece. Its available field positions feed PhotonVision's simulated targets. Its
intake event changes the possession IO. Its launch operation consumes the held piece and adds the
projectile. The robot state machine reads camera and possession inputs; it does not query the
world's exact piece location to decide where to drive.

FuelSim does not synchronize PhotonVision targets automatically. The student writes that adapter and
updates it from the same world after each physics step. Remove collected targets and do not leave a
duplicate piece visible after acquisition. A missed projectile may return to the field according to
the model; it is not automatically held again.

Use measured flywheel speed and measured pivot angle at launch. Record the pitch convention, radius,
slip assumption, launch height, and field-relative robot velocity. Keep a scored count distinct from
held state and launch count. Mirror relevant world state into the saved log so another student can
reconstruct the event.

## Start from Cobra's Autonomous

In the fork, `AutonomousManager` builds Cobra's routines from fixed BLine paths, intake deploy and
stow commands that wait until the pivot is near its goal, and timed shooting segments such as
`handleInterpolateShooterToHub` from Step 1. `BLineManager` shows how Cobra constructs the BLine
follower and its PID controllers. No routine reads camera observations, and nothing confirms
possession: collection is assumed from the path and the clock.

The one-piece sequence replaces that assumption with observed states. Write down, for each state,
the measurements that permit its transition. Route normal forward-feed requests through the
readiness policy from Step 9. Keep the mechanism library to four explicit families, with commands
owned by parent subsystems.

Build seven integrated runs: one launch, the four failure conditions below, and interruption at two
points. Save each run as a named WPILOG. Inspect state, refusal, camera and possession inputs,
measured mechanism values, estimate, inventory, launch count, and score together. A passing launch
test does not establish that the projectile scored or that the physical robot is tuned. Preserve
misses while calibrating the model against physical observations.

## Required Failure Runs

| Case | Injection | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No visible piece | Remove or hide the piece before the search. | Search ends within its bound; no possession or launch is invented. |
| Target Lost | Stop observations before collection. | The stale-target rule is visible and the attempt stops or recovers as documented. |
| Collection Timeout | Prevent possession confirmation at the attempted pickup. | The bounded attempt fails; held and scored remain false. |
| Never Ready | Keep measured speed or angle outside tolerance. | The feeder stays stopped and readiness times out. |
| Interruption | Cancel once during driving and once during scoring. | Drive and feeder stop; the piece is neither duplicated nor consumed twice. |

The mentor's transition-policy tests illustrate these cases; the student demonstrates them through
the integrated robot and actual IO path. Repeat any failure runs affected when FuelSim replaces the
initial field stand-in.

## Independent Change

The mentor changes the initial piece location within the camera's supported region. The student
predicts the acquisition pose, runs the full sequence, and explains any difference using
observations, path error, possession, readiness, and score. Save the failure logs alongside the
successful run.

## Score Under Declared Assumptions

B3-12.1 and B3-12.7 close only when the integrated run demonstrates a score under declared geometry
and model assumptions; collection and a launch alone do not complete them. Use the shot calibration
and the commissioning record to reconcile measured release speed, geometry, and the preserved
empirical tables. Keep held-out validation shots separate from fitting.
The legacy Step 6 scoring experiment and dropped-ball scoring fixtures remain useful scoped tests;
neither establishes Cobra's shot calibration. Other coursework may continue while this evidence is
pending. Do not change empirical table values solely to turn a simulation assertion green.

## Keep Arrival and Visibility Checks Consistent

Give the follower a small translation tolerance to reach the intake pickup region; 3 in works in
simulation. Within a short distance and heading of the pickup goal, such as 6 in and 8 degrees,
acquisition can finish the bounded final approach using estimated pose while the intake hides the
target. Arrival alone never confirms possession. Repeat the collection-timeout and launch runs with
added loop delay, for example 10 ms: the timeout must still occur when possession never arrives, and
one confirmed launch must still occur when it does. Keep the lost-target test separate. Each failure
test checks for the terminal reason of the condition it demonstrates.

## Badge Check

S means simulation, H means hardware, W means written reasoning, and M means mentor-observed review.
The student records an evidence link, an explanation in the student's own words, the mentor's
signature, and the date for each row. The mentor asks the
student to explain a change and its result before signing.

| Row | Work | Done When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B3-12.1 | Baseline one-piece sequence | S: Start at a specified pose, travel to a search region, detect through PhotonVision, approach using BLine, confirm collection through simulated inputs, return, wait for readiness and execute scoring. Log explicit available/held/scored state. |
| B3-12.2 | No visible piece | S: Record a bounded failed attempt and defined stopped or recovery state without inventing possession. |
| B3-12.3 | Target lost | S: Lose the observation before collection; apply the documented stale-target rule and log the outcome. |
| B3-12.4 | Collection timeout | S: Reach the attempted collection without confirming possession; timeout without marking a piece held or scored. |
| B3-12.5 | Never ready | S: Hold a mechanism outside readiness; terminate or recover within a stated bound, without launching. |
| B3-12.6 | Interruption | S: Interrupt during driving and during scoring; verify documented safe outputs and consistent piece state. |
| B3-12.7 | Integrate FuelSim | S/W: Reuse the flywheel experiment with measured pivot position. One field model owns the piece; adapt its representation into PhotonVision and possession IO. Consume one held piece once and use the simulator's scoring result. Recheck failure cases affected by the integration. |

## For the Mentor

`Badge3MentorSolution` maps its states in `frc/robot/subsystems/acquisition/Acquisition.java` to
their measurements in `ONE-PIECE.md`. `OnePieceScenario` runs the seven cases (`SUCCESS`,
`NO_PIECE`, `TARGET_LOST`, `COLLECTION_TIMEOUT`, `NEVER_READY`, `CANCEL_DRIVE`, and `CANCEL_FEED`)
and saves their logs under `build/evidence`; `SlowCollectionSimulationTest` and
`SlowSuccessSimulationTest` repeat two of them with 10 ms of added loop delay. The reference's
`SUCCESS` run and its slow variant establish collection and launch with one launch and zero scores,
so the reference itself has not yet demonstrated a score. `SHOT-CALIBRATION.md` holds the
calibration worksheet.
