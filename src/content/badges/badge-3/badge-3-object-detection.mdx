The student builds a camera input path for one stationary game piece and uses observations to choose
an acquisition pose. Start from the Badge 2 pose and AprilTag work. Observation IO and geometry can
proceed alongside mechanism refinement; Step 12 requires this module and the subsystem states
together. Read [Pose Geometry](/frc/pose-geometry), [PhotonVision](/frc/photonvision),
[Vision Simulation](/frc/vision-simulation), and Cobra's object-detection IO and tracker linked
below.

## Build the IO Boundary

Start from Cobra's `ObjectDetectionIO`, `ObjectDetectionIOPhoton`, and `ObjectDetectionIOPhotonSim`
in the fork. `ObjectDetectionIOPhoton` already reads `getAllUnreadResults()` and stamps each
observation with its result timestamp, and `FuelTracker` forgets observations older than a tunable
tracking memory. The simulated implementation extends the real implementation and supplies a
`PhotonCameraSim`, following the same pattern used for AprilTags. Both modes consume PhotonVision
results through the real parsing path. REPLAY reads recorded inputs without constructing cameras or
field physics.

The input record contains connection state, observation timestamps, target yaw and pitch, available
class/confidence information, and rejection reasons. Process unread results once. An empty result
means no target in that frame; no new frame means the age of the previous observation continues
increasing. Clear or expire targets deliberately rather than treating an old result as current.

The provided coprocessor and color camera already run PhotonVision with a working pipeline.
Installation and model training are supplied setup. The student owns the robot-side IO, transform
configuration, validity rules, tracking and selection.

## Turn a Measurement into a Location

A camera bearing identifies a ray, not a complete three-dimensional position. For the stationary
floor-piece exercise, a known target-center height provides the missing constraint. Intersect the
transformed camera ray with that height plane, reject nearly parallel or backward intersections, and
bound the accepted distance. Record the assumptions that make the estimate possible.

Use `FieldToRobot`, `RobotToCamera`, and `CameraToPiece` names when drawing the transforms. Use the
robot pose at the observation timestamp when history is available. If the exercise uses a stationary
robot to avoid motion compensation, state that limitation. The same calculation with the current
pose on a moving robot needs a latency-error analysis.

The guided example checks a known target ahead of the camera, then moves it to the side. The student
predicts the sign of yaw, verifies the library's yaw convention, and compares estimated field
position with the configured simulation position. Log the estimate and truth separately. The
selection logic sees only the estimate.

## Prepare for Moving Observations

The stationary physical camera check establishes geometry without demonstrating motion compensation.
Before Step 12, the student documents how observations are related to robot pose during the
simulated approach. Use pose history at capture time when available, or stop and obtain a fresh
stationary observation before calculating a field target. State the permitted observation age and
motion conditions; reject frames outside them. A current-pose calculation on a moving robot requires
a quantified latency-error bound and a justified acceptance limit.

Test a delayed frame during simulated motion and show the compensation or rejection decision in the
log. This bridge belongs to the observation evidence in B3-10.2. The target remains a stationary
piece; the robot may move.

## Select an Acquisition Pose

Track the single stationary piece with an expiry time. Select a valid target using a documented
rule. Calculate a robot goal that puts the intake at the piece, including the robot-to-intake offset
and desired heading. Driving the robot center onto the piece is not equivalent to aligning the
intake.

Test no target, stale target, out-of-bounds position, near-horizontal ray, and a deliberately wrong
mounting transform. Save both accepted and rejected observations. The mentor chooses a different
camera transform for an independent prediction and correction.

## Physical Camera Check

Use the provided Orange Pi 5 or Rubik Pi 3 and camera with the mentor present. Measure the mounting
transform and place the stationary target at several known locations. Compare observations and
calculated positions with tape measurements. Record lighting, pipeline, target type, distances,
timestamp behavior and limitations. This demonstration does not require autonomous motion on
hardware.

## Replace Cobra's Distance Estimate

Cobra's `ObjectDetectionIOPhoton` estimates distance from the target's pitch with
`PhotonUtils.calculateDistanceToTargetMeters`, divides by the cosine of yaw, and places the result
from the current robot pose. Replace that with a ray intersection: transform the full camera ray
into the field with the capture-time planar robot pose and the configured camera translation and
rotation, then intersect the fuel-center plane. FuelSim's fuel radius is 0.075 m (about 3 in), so a
grounded fuel's center sits that high. Bound the accepted distance, for example to 10 m (about 33
ft). Reject nonfinite values, an invalid camera height, bearings at or beyond 89 degrees, nearly
parallel rays, backward intersections, excessive range, and points outside the field. A plausible
but inaccurate mounting transform can still produce a plausible wrong answer; known-location
measurements are needed to detect it.

Log each frame's timestamp, sequence number and reason, and each observation's raw bearings,
class/confidence, frame index and reason. Use -1 for an unavailable class or confidence. Keep three
frame cases distinct: no new frame, a received image with no targets, and a frame without
capture-pose history. The last is a rejection, not a target at a placeholder zero pose. Test the
parser with recorded packets and check that the raw inputs round-trip through the log.

Normal REPLAY uses saved projected inputs. To investigate a projection change, recompute from the
saved raw bearings, frame-indexed capture pose, camera transform, height, and range in a separate
diagnostic comparison. Older logs without these fields cannot support that raw-bearing analysis.

## Heading Assistance Checkpoint

After single-piece tracking, start from Cobra's driver controller A-button binding (port 0). It runs
`DriveCommands.joystickDriveAtAngle` toward `BobotState.getFuelClusterVARC().getRotationTarget()`
while the driver keeps translation control. Find what that target is when no cluster is observed.
Keep driver translation active while heading turns toward the selected observed group, document the
selection rule and its tie-break, and demonstrate selection, loss fallback, and release stop. Save
the comparison with B3-10.2. This checkpoint requires heading assistance; driving automatically to a
cluster is outside the required work.

## Model the Camera in Simulation

Start the camera model with grounded fuel only. Airborne fuel is excluded because the projection
assumes a fuel center 0.075 m (about 3 in) above the floor. This is a simulation simplification, not
a robot-side height classifier. Captured images keep their original scene and timestamp while
waiting for delivery. Motion or collection after capture cannot rewrite an older image.

Test moving fuel and robot poses through PhotonCamera, the ordinary object-detection IO, and the
normal Drive estimator history. Test the A-button binding for heading alignment, driver translation,
and target loss. Approximate spatial tracks can merge close balls; their count is not a proven ball
count.

## For the Mentor

`Badge3MentorSolution` replaces Cobra's distance estimate with `FuelProjection`, and
`CAMERA-PROJECTION.md` explains it. Its IO logs `frameTimestamps`, `frameSequences`, `frameReasons`,
`observationFrameIndexes`, and `observationReasons`, with the frame reasons `EMPTY_FRAME` and
`NO_CAPTURE_POSE`. `ProjectionContractTest` checks the parser and log round trip,
`MovingCameraProjectionTest` exercises the camera queue and capture-pose history, and
`DriverAssistSimulationTest` operates the A-button binding. `CLUSTER-ASSIST.md` documents the
nearest-group tie-break and switching margin. The legacy `curriculum-reference` names its adapters
`ObjectDetectionIOReal` and `ObjectDetectionIOSim`; these are the same roles.
