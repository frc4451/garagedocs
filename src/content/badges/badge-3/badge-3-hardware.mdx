Step 13 reviews the physical evidence collected throughout the badge. Motor controller setup and use
begin in [Badge 2 Hardware Setup and Utilization](/badges/badge-2-hardware). Each advanced
demonstration follows its mechanism's simulation rows in Steps 4, 5 and 7 and the fixture
preparation below. The physical work happens alongside each mechanism, and this step reviews it.
Steps 4, 5, 7 and 10 send the student here before their hardware rows: read the setup sheet and
first-enable sections before the first powered check.

## Complete the Setup Sheet

Update the [Badge 2 setup sheet](/badges/badge-2-hardware#complete-the-setup-sheet) for each actual
arrangement. Record configuration revision, motor and motor controller, bus and identity, feedback,
gearing, current/output bounds, license requirements, reference procedure, permitted travel and
support/clearance. Add the selected output mode, gain units, profile constraints, and error,
settling, overshoot and repeatability targets for the advanced experiment.

The commissioning record is part of the setup sheet. It holds the installed sensor and reference
values below. Record distances in inches and note the sensor's meter reading beside each one, since
the CANrange reports meters.

| Item | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Possession sensor | CANrange CAN ID and bus, mounting location, and what a detection proves about the game piece. |
| Thresholds | Occupied and empty distance ranges, signal strength in each state, and the band between them. |
| Health and freshness | Measurement health when empty and occupied, update rate, sample age, and a disconnect and reconnect log. |
| Pivot references | Manual reference procedure, absolute sensor offset, and the repeatability result. |
| Gains | Each output mode's gains with their units. |

The student fills in the possession rows (sensor, thresholds, and health and freshness) before the
physical B3-04.2 test in Step 4; this step reviews them. SIM evidence does not supply these
installed values. Until these values are reviewed and entered, check that the robot's possession
input reports invalid on hardware, so the Step 9 readiness policy refuses a normal feed. A
disconnected or invalid sensor reading must have the same effect.

## First Enable

Follow the [first-enable procedure](/badges/badge-2-hardware#first-enable) for any fixture that has
changed since its Badge 2 enable. Review the selected Badge 2 physical route and both CTRE
simulation paths. A student with matching physical FX evidence can reuse its physical voltage
baseline after verifying the present configuration. A student who used FXS or Nova physically
establishes a bounded FX physical voltage baseline with the mentor here, as preparation for the
advanced experiments.

Before a torque-current comparison, show positive/reverse voltage, feedback direction, stop,
interruption and disable on the actual FX fixture. Save the configuration and physical log with
B3-13.1. For a gravity-loaded pivot, establish support, reference validity and travel limits before
normal motion.

## Required Demonstrations

`TorqueCurrentFOC` and `FusedCANcoder` feedback need a Phoenix Pro license on the device or on its
CANivore; simulation needs none. The two behave differently on an unlicensed Talon FX. A torque
request that passed every simulation row can produce no motion: CTRE documents that the device
disables its output, sets the `UnlicensedFeatureInUse` fault and blinks its unlicensed pattern. A
device configured for `FusedCANcoder` falls back to `RemoteCANcoder` feedback and sets the
`UsingFusedCANcoderWhileUnlicensed` fault. Confirm license state in Phoenix Tuner X before the
appointment. Check `UnlicensedFeatureInUse` first when a torque request does nothing, and
`UsingFusedCANcoderWhileUnlicensed` on a pivot configured for fused feedback.

Row B3-13.1 requires physical torque-current roller, flywheel and pivot demonstrations. On at least
one mechanism, compare voltage and torque-current control using a verified matching Badge 2 FX
physical voltage baseline or a new matched voltage run. Record separate gain units and comparable
conditions.

Run each demonstration through the student's own IO on the deployed robot code, not through Tuner X
controls. Make each log identify the active request type and its gain units. Before the voltage
and torque-current comparison, predict how measured speed and supply current change in each mode
when the same load is added, then compare the prediction with the log.

Rows B3-05.2 and B3-07.2 to B3-07.4 assess the physical characterization and pivot reference checks;
this step reviews them with B3-13.1. Complete physical characterization only within its approved
effort, speed and duration bounds, and show cancellation cleanup. On the pivot, also complete the
manual reference, absolute or fused reference, travel and repeatability checks.

Badge 2 introduced all three motor controllers. The mentor reviews the student's Badge 2 physical
route as prior evidence: FX under B3-13.1, FXS under B3-13.2 and Nova under B3-13.3. B3-13.2 and
B3-13.3 are optional extensions when that motor controller was not supplied in Badge 2. The Talon FX
and Talon FXS CTRE simulation comparisons for the roller, flywheel and pivot in Steps 4, 5 and 7
remain required and extend the earlier voltage work.

For a Nova route or extension, use the [controller introduction](/hardware/thrifty-nova-and-motors),
a documented motor and feedback arrangement, and the ThriftyLib version the project pins. The
Starter pins 2026.0.1 (`com.thethriftybot`); the fork moves to 2026.2.2 (`com.thrifty`) when the
library is copied in Step 1. Retain configuration, conversion, output-boundary, status and stop
evidence. A general WPILib model does not emulate Nova firmware. Cobra's `SingleRollerIONova` is a
design reference to inspect, not a fixture prescription; verify its assumptions against the selected
device.

The CANrange presence reading must change the roller subsystem's behavior during a physical presence
test, in the way stated for B3-04.2 in Step 4. The camera demonstration in Step 10 uses stationary
observations. Physical autonomous collection and scoring, intentional jams, and injected electrical
faults are not required.

## Independent Handoff

Another member follows the written setup and reference procedure with the mentor present. The
student who wrote the procedure observes and answers questions only after the attempt, then corrects
any unclear instructions. The other member then repeats the affected action.

The handoff supports B3-07.2, B3-13.4 and B3-15.1, but a required hardware row remains open until
its demonstration is complete. In Step 15, reuse this observed handoff for B3-15.1 when it covers
that row; repeat only missing or changed actions.

## Badge Check

H means hardware, W means written reasoning, and M means mentor-observed review. The student records
an evidence link and an explanation in the student's own words for each row in the fork's README.
The mentor asks the student to explain a change and its result, checks each row, then signs and
dates the step on the [sign-off sheet](/documents/badges/badge-3/badge-3-sign-off.pdf).

| Row | Work | Done When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B3-13.1 | Talon FX hardware | H/M: Verify or establish the FX physical voltage baseline, then demonstrate torque-current roller, flywheel and pivot. Compare voltage and torque-current behavior on at least one of them. |
| B3-13.2 | Talon FXS hardware | H/M: Prior-evidence review when FXS was supplied in Badge 2; otherwise an optional physical extension. Review or complete a configured physical mechanism, voltage operation, logged feedback, stop, interruption and disable. |
| B3-13.3 | Thrifty Nova adaptation | H/W/M: Prior-evidence review when Nova was supplied in Badge 2; otherwise an optional physical extension. Review or complete one supported physical arrangement with verified configuration, conversion, voltage boundary, status, stop, interruption and disable. No additional motor matrix is required. |
| B3-13.4 | Hardware handoff data | W/M: Record actual device IDs, bus, licenses, motor, gearing, encoder, travel, limits, gains and repeatable operating procedure. Distinguish fixture values from example values. |
