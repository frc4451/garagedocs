Badge 1 is for first-year Automation Team students, including ninth graders who have never programmed. You can ask questions, use the linked lessons, and practice with a mentor. At a sign-off, you operate the tools and explain your choices.

By the end, you will be able to:

1. Save and share code with Git and GitHub.
2. Explain true-or-false conditions, including modus ponens and modus tollens.
3. Write the Java used by the Kit Bot lessons.
4. Build and test a simulated Kit Bot.
5. Configure and demonstrate a real Kit Bot with a mentor supervising hardware safety.

### The steps

| Step | Work | What you show |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Pretest](/badges/badge-1-pretest) | Your starting knowledge; there is no pass score |
| 2 | [Accounts and Tools](/badges/badge-1-tools) | Open your tools and build a WPILib project |
| 3 | [Git](/badges/badge-1-git) | Save changes, use a branch, and complete a reviewed pull request |
| 4 | [Boolean Logic](/badges/badge-1-boolean-logic) | Complete and correct the paper worksheet; explain two arguments |
| 5 | [Java Fundamentals](/badges/badge-1-java) | Complete the required Practice assignments and explain your code |
| 6 | [Kit Bot in Simulation](/badges/badge-1-simulation) | Run the capstone from a fresh copy of your repository |
| 7 | [Kit Bot on the Robot](/badges/badge-1-hardware) | Configure and demonstrate the physical robot with a mentor |
| Review | Repeat the [pretest](/badges/badge-1-pretest) | Compare what you can explain now with your first answers |

Follow the numbered steps. Step 2 opens R00 only to prepare the tools. The full Kit Bot sequence starts in Step 6. Simulation lets you find code problems before a hardware session.

### How the sheet works

Print the [sign-off sheet in Google Docs](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1FHhinkTDgvzAyIR0UuoW-VRv7J8d-mUgtN3Slgaeesw/edit) and the [Boolean Logic worksheet in Google Docs](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1WFRUK9Oz_xbyb7OJYDED7Dt4KJtrxJgEF3IeMDeb1Sk/edit). Keep both papers and bring them to each check. A mentor signs after watching the result named in that row.

Keep Java assignments in `garagedocs-practice` and robot code in a separate Kit Bot repository. **Evidence** means something you can show: a commit, a running example, a graph, a worksheet, or a demonstration. It is not a separate digital badge account.

Work can span several meetings. If one check needs more practice, keep the completed signatures and agree with your mentor on what to try before repeating that check. You may use notes and documentation unless a check specifically says otherwise.

### Before and after

Take the pretest before lessons and repeat it after the capstone. Blank answers are allowed. The scores help you and your mentor choose what to review; they do not decide whether you may begin.

The simulation capstone can be completed without a robot. **Badge 1 also requires Step 7.** If hardware is unavailable, keep Step 7 pending and retain all completed work.
