Badge 1 is for first-year Automation Team students, including students who have never programmed.
The student can ask questions, use the linked lessons, and practice with a mentor or Automation Team Lead. At a sign-off, the student operates the tools and explains their choices to a mentor.

By the end, the student can:

1. Save and share code with Git and GitHub.
2. Explain true-or-false conditions and logical implications.
3. Write Java used by the Kit Bot lessons.
4. Build and test a simulated Kit Bot.
5. Configure and demonstrate a real Kit Bot with a mentor supervising hardware safety.

### The steps

| Step | Work | What the student shows |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Pretest](/badges/badge-1-pretest) | Starting assessment; there is no pass score |
| 2 | [Accounts and Tools](/badges/badge-1-tools) | Open the tools and build a WPILib project |
| 3 | [Git](/badges/badge-1-git) | Save changes, use a branch, and complete a reviewed pull request |
| 4 | [Boolean Logic](/badges/badge-1-boolean-logic) | Complete and correct the paper worksheet |
| 5 | [Java Fundamentals](/badges/badge-1-java) | Complete the required Practice assignments and explain the code |
| 6 | [Kit Bot in Simulation](/badges/badge-1-simulation) | Run the capstone from a fresh clone of the Kit Bot repository |
| 7 | [Kit Bot on the Robot](/badges/badge-1-hardware) | Configure and demonstrate the physical robot with a mentor |
| Review | Repeat the [pretest](/badges/badge-1-pretest) | Score 18 of 20 (90%) or higher, and compare with the first sitting |

The steps are meant to run in order. Step 4 can be completed without a computer, and every step except Step 7 can be completed without a robot.

### How the sign-off sheet works

The student receives a copy of the [sign-off sheet in Google Docs](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1FHhinkTDgvzAyIR0UuoW-VRv7J8d-mUgtN3Slgaeesw/edit) and the [Boolean Logic worksheet in Google Docs](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1WFRUK9Oz_xbyb7OJYDED7Dt4KJtrxJgEF3IeMDeb1Sk/edit), keeps both papers, and brings them to each check. The student is responsible for their own sheets. A mentor signs after watching the result named in that row.

Badge 1 uses two repositories: `<yourname>Badge01` for Steps 2 through 5, and a separate Kit Bot repository for robot code. **Evidence** means something the student can show: a commit, a running example, a graph, a worksheet, or a demonstration. The signed sheet is the record; there is no digital badge account.

Work can span several meetings. If one check needs more practice, the completed signatures stand, and the student and mentor agree on what to try before repeating that check. Notes and documentation are allowed unless a check says otherwise.

### How the pretest and review work

The student takes the pretest before any lesson and repeats it after the capstone. Blank answers are allowed in the first sitting.
The scores help the student and mentor choose what to review and guide the individual curriculum; the first sitting does not decide whether the student may begin. The second sitting is graded, and 18 of 20 (90%) or higher is expected.

### How the capstone works

The goal is a recreation of the 2026 Kit Bot (FRC 4864's *Scorpion*) in the student's own GitHub account, built in simulation before it is tested on a real robot. Most logic problems surface in simulation before the student touches the physical robot.
The simulation capstone can be completed without a robot. **Badge 1 also requires Step 7.** If hardware is unavailable, Step 7 stays pending and all completed work stands.
