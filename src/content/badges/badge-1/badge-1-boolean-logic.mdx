Step 4 teaches how to combine true-or-false conditions and decide what follows from given facts.
The student works through the lesson in class or at home, then completes the paper worksheet.

### Required work

The student reads [Boolean Logic](/java/fundamentals/boolean-logic), then completes the worksheet below.

### Boolean Logic worksheet

The [Boolean Logic worksheet (PDF)](/documents/badges/badge-1/badge-1-boolean-logic-worksheet.pdf)
is printed and completed on paper. 
It has five sections, each with its definitions beside it: 
- statements
- truth tables
- equivalent expressions
- if-then statements
- rules for drawing conclusions

The student may use the lesson and _only_ their written notes, then reviews and corrects the answers with a mentor.

Two of the rules have Latin names the lesson defines. 
1) Modus ponens
    - if p then q, and p, therefore, q. 
2) Modus tollens
    - if p then q, and not q, therefore, not p.
    
The student explains the worksheet's modus ponens and modus tollens problems in their own words.

### Badge check

The mentor signs this step when:

1. Every worksheet section is complete and corrected.
2. The student can explain why the conclusion follows in the modus ponens problem.
3. The student can explain why the conclusion follows in the modus tollens problem.

### Programming Math after Badge 1

[Discrete Math Foundations](/java/fundamentals/discrete-math) expands this lesson into sets, relations, functions, counting, probability and graphs. Badge 1 does not require that full lesson. [Vector Math](/java/fundamentals/vector-math) and [Matrix Math](/java/fundamentals/matrix-math) are also later material.
