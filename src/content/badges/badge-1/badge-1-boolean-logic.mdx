Step 4 teaches the logic behind robot conditions before those conditions appear throughout the Java and Kit Bot work. Students complete this as a classroom lesson and worksheet, so the requirement is visible instead of being hidden inside the Java check.

<RulesBox title="Before you start">

[Step 3](/badges/badge-1-git) complete. This step does not assume that you already know Java. The examples use short robot conditions, and the lesson explains each operator before asking you to use it.

</RulesBox>

### Required work

1. Read [Boolean Logic](/java/fundamentals/java-boolean-logic).
2. Complete the [Badge 1 Boolean Logic worksheet](/badges/badge-1-sign-off#badge-1-boolean-logic-worksheet) on paper without running code.
3. Review each answer with a mentor and correct any mistakes.
4. Explain the modus ponens and modus tollens problems in your own words.

The worksheet also checks propositions and predicates, truth tables, De Morgan's laws, tautologies and contradictions, implication, the converse, inverse and contrapositive, hypothetical syllogism, and disjunctive syllogism.

### Badge check

The mentor signs this step when:

1. Every worksheet section is complete and corrected.
2. The student can explain why the conclusion follows in the modus ponens problem.
3. The student can explain why the conclusion follows in the modus tollens problem.

The student does not need to memorize the Latin names. They do need to recognize each argument and explain why it is valid.

### Programming Math after Badge 1

[Discrete Math Foundations](/java/fundamentals/java-discrete-math) expands this lesson into sets, relations, functions, counting, probability and graphs. Badge 1 does not require that full lesson. [Vector Math](/java/fundamentals/java-vector-math) and [Matrix Math](/java/fundamentals/java-matrix-math) are also later material.
