Step 11 starts with a guided PathPlanner comparison on the differential Kit Bot, then brings the
Step 9 mechanisms and the controller class into `Badge2Swerve` and builds two complete swerve
routines there: one with PathPlanner and one with BLine. Each required swerve routine follows at
least three named paths and runs at least two mechanism actions. The student records route and
action intent for the 2026 field. BLine is carried forward to Badge 3.

The mechanism actions on the Kit Bot come from the template's `Superstructure`; on the swerve robot
they come from the composed subsystems of
[Step 9: Mechanism Composition](/badges/badge-2-mechanism-composition), built in `Badge2Bench` and
copied into `Badge2Swerve` below, which is why composition comes first.

Before moving from the Kit Bot to swerve, work through
[Drivetrain Fundamentals](/frc/tank-and-swerve), ending with
[Drivetrain Checks](/frc/drivetrain-checks), as assigned in
[Step 10: Pose, Frames, and Odometry](/badges/badge-2-math#tank-to-swerve). The left/right speed
calculation becomes a speed-and-angle calculation for each module; a path follower supplies the
chassis motion that feeds those calculations.

## Guided Example

With the mentor, confirm the differential configuration and run one bounded path. Compare its travel
and heading with the swerve behavior later in this step. The full three-path differential routine is
an optional extension.

### PathPlanner on the Kit Bot

The AdvantageKit 2026 Kit Bot template inherits the differential-drive template's PathPlanner
configuration. Its `Drive` constructor calls `AutoBuilder.configure` with the pose supplier, the
pose reset, a robot-relative `ChassisSpeeds` supplier built from the left and right wheel velocities
through `DifferentialDriveKinematics`, a `runClosedLoop(ChassisSpeeds)` consumer, a
`PPLTVController(0.02, maxSpeedMetersPerSec)`, the `ppConfig` from `DriveConstants`, and an alliance
flip supplier. `runClosedLoop` converts the chassis speeds to wheel speeds, computes a `kS` and `kV`
feedforward for each side, and hands velocity and feedforward to the IO. The
[PathPlanner](/frc/pathplanner#autobuilder-configuration-differential) lesson has the differential
configuration beside the swerve one.

`PPLTVController` is the follower for a drivetrain that cannot move sideways: it controls forward
speed and turn rate, and the robot's heading is the direction of travel. `DriveConstants` carries
the numbers `RobotConfig` is built from: mass, moment of inertia, wheel coefficient of friction,
wheel radius, gearing, current limit, motors per side and track width. The template ships with
defaults. The simulated Kit Bot comes from WPILib's `DifferentialDrivetrainSim.createKitbotSim` with
two CIMs per side, 10.71:1 gearing and six-inch wheels, so the student can confirm motors per side,
gearing and wheel size against it. Mass and moment of inertia are computed inside that WPILib model;
the student identifies the `ppConfig` values for them as template defaults.

Install the PathPlanner app before drawing the first path, as the
[PathPlanner lesson](/frc/pathplanner#install-the-pathplanner-app) describes, and open the
`Badge2KitBot` project folder in it. In the app's
[Robot Config](https://pathplanner.dev/robot-config.html) settings, leave holonomic mode off; the
track width setting appears only for a non-holonomic robot. `AutoBuilder` follows paths with the
`RobotConfig` built in code from `DriveConstants`. The app's settings, saved in
`src/main/deploy/pathplanner/settings.json`, shape only the editor's preview, and the template's
copy does not match `DriveConstants`. Enter the code's values in the app so the preview matches what
the robot runs.

## Independent Change

Move the workflow to swerve, then keep the same drivetrain interfaces and mechanism commands while
replacing the PathPlanner follower with BLine. The student chooses the routes, action timing, and
the path that demonstrates heading independent of travel.

### Bring the Mechanisms Into Badge2Swerve

The mechanisms were built and tested in `Badge2Bench`. The swerve routines need them in
`Badge2Swerve`, so the student copies them across before drawing a swerve path. Keep package names
unchanged, and copy:

- the Step 9 parent subsystems, their components, IO interfaces, `@AutoLog` inputs, general
  simulation IO and configuration records, with their tests;
- shared helpers they use, such as the `SimBattery` owner, each model's load registration, and the
  `SimBattery.update()` call in `Robot.simulationPeriodic()`;
- the controller class from Step 6, and the Step 7 and Step 9 bindings for the composed parents;
- the Lombok plugin line in `build.gradle`.

Leave the physical IO and ThriftyLib in `Badge2Bench`; `Badge2Swerve` never runs the fixture and
keeps its template's own Phoenix dependency. In `Badge2Swerve`'s `RobotContainer`, construct each
mechanism's general simulation IO in `SIM` and its empty IO in `REPLAY`. Only `Badge2Bench` runs in
`REAL`; all physical hardware work happens on the bench, and `Badge2Swerve` is never deployed in
Badge 2. The copied mechanisms therefore need no `REAL` IO in this project. Record the `Badge2Bench`
revision that was copied in the README. A later mechanism change is made in `Badge2Bench` first,
tested there, and copied again, so the two copies do not drift apart. Run the copied tests in
`Badge2Swerve` before continuing.

The battery exercise covers the copied mechanism models. Register each load once and update the
supply once per simulation loop. The supplied swerve `ModuleIOSim` uses fixed-voltage inputs;
drivetrain battery sag is outside Badge 2. Scheduler-only tests call the battery update themselves
because they do not run `Robot.simulationPeriodic()`.

### Drive With the Controller

The swerve template drives from a plain `CommandXboxController` in `RobotContainer`, and its
`DriveCommands.joystickDrive` applies its own input shaping: `getLinearVelocityFromJoysticks`
applies a 0.1 deadband to the stick's magnitude and then squares it, and the rotation axis gets a
deadband and signed squaring of its own. Passing the student's processed getters into that command
would deadband and square the same input twice.

All input shaping moves into the student's controller class:

1. Replace the template's `CommandXboxController` field with a `BadgeCommandXboxController` on the
   same port.
2. In `getLinearVelocityFromJoysticks`, remove the `MathUtil.applyDeadband` call and the squaring
   line, so the method only converts the two stick values into a direction and magnitude.
3. In `joystickDrive`, remove the rotation deadband and the signed-squaring line, so `omega` is the
   supplied value.
4. Pass `() -> -controller.getLeftYSquared()` and `() -> -controller.getLeftXSquared()` where the
   template passed `getLeftY()` and `getLeftX()`, in both the `joystickDrive` default command and
   the `joystickDriveAtAngle` binding, and `() -> -controller.getRightXSquared()` where
   `joystickDrive` received `getRightX()`. The minus signs stay; they convert the stick's axes to
   the field's and make pushing right X to the right turn the robot clockwise.

The template shaped the stick's magnitude; the controller class shapes each axis separately. On a
diagonal, each axis crosses the deadband on its own, so a small diagonal push can move the robot
along one axis only. Drive the simulated chassis in both directions on each axis and diagonally, and
record what changed.

### PathPlanner on Swerve

The same file workflow, on the swerve template in `Badge2Swerve`. The template's `Drive` configures
`AutoBuilder` with `PPHolonomicDriveController` and a `RobotConfig` built from `TunerConstants`:
module locations from `getModuleTranslations()`, wheel radius, drive gear ratio, slip current and
the speed at 12 volts. The holonomic controller commands translation and rotation separately, which
is the behavior a differential drivetrain cannot offer and the reason for row 10.14.

Open the `Badge2Swerve` project folder in the PathPlanner app. In its
[Robot Config](https://pathplanner.dev/robot-config.html) settings, turn holonomic mode on and enter
the module and drive values from `TunerConstants`. As on the Kit Bot, `AutoBuilder` uses the
`RobotConfig` built in code, and the template's `settings.json` only shapes the editor's preview.

Before either swerve routine runs, the student defines an overall time bound and a stopped end
state. A timeout is recorded separately from successful arrival. Interruption stops drive and leaves
the Step 9 mechanisms in their documented safe states. Path events use those parent commands rather
than writing motor IO directly. Follow the [bounded mechanism-action example](/frc/bline#events):
events change a flag, and a command inside the routine's deadline observes it. Check normal
completion, timeout and cancellation while enabled, disable, and a second run. No action or event
flag from the previous run remains active.

## Badge Check

<StepsBox title="Path Following Badge Check">

1. The student explains the guided Kit Bot comparison and the two complete 2026 swerve routines. The
   mentor reviews the comparison log, then runs PathPlanner and BLine on swerve. Each run starts
   from a documented simulation state. The nominal run resets pose once at the routine's start; the
   BLine offset check below preserves its offset instead.
2. For each swerve run, AdvantageScope shows the planned and measured poses on the 2D field and the
   mechanism goals on a graph.
3. The mentor opens each swerve log and asks the student to point at the start and end of every path
   and every action, and to say what changed at each transition.
4. The mentor switches the alliance in the Sim GUI and reruns one routine. It mirrors correctly.
5. For the BLine routine, the mentor sets a starting offset and watches the robot recover the line.

</StepsBox>
