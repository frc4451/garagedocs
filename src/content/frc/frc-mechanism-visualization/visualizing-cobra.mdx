Use the mentor-supplied Badge 3 projects for this exercise. `Badge3Starter` preserves the original
Cobra baseline; `Badge3MentorSolution` is the mentor migration. Work in your own fork and keep the
source/version record. The completed `Badge2MechanismReference` supplies numeric logging, physics,
and the Badge 2 2D drawings. Complete the 2D drawing checks in
[Step 8: Logged Mechanism and CAD Visualization](/badges/badge-3-logged-mechanisms) before adding
Cobra's CAD.

## Start with One Measured Motion

Read `org/robotzgarage/frc2027/mechanisms/README.md` in the reference. Follow one intake request
from the parent command to `Roller`, `RollerIO`, and the selected adapter. Then trace one
measurement back to the drawing. Commands belong to the parent subsystem; the drawing receives
values and displays them.

| Family | What to draw | What to explain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roller | A spinner driven by measured speed | Direction and motion, without inventing a speed goal |
| Flywheel | A spinner and measured-versus-goal speed trace | Readiness requires connection, an active goal, and measured speed |
| Pivot | A ligament at measured mechanism angle | The measurement reference, limits, and difference from the goal |
| Elevator | A carriage at measured height | Meters, drum conversion, and travel limits; this reference stays simulated |

`IntakeMechanismDrawing` in Badge3MentorSolution supplies the measured/goal pivot example.
The other drawing families in the table are extensions. In Cobra, intake and feeder use rollers,
the shooter uses a flywheel, and the intake arm and hood use pivots. The reusable components stay
explicit; you do not need a universal mechanism class to combine them.

Keep measured, controller-reference, and requested-goal traces distinct. A model driven directly by
the goal can look correct while a motor is stopped. Inspect the trace as well as the picture.

## Keep the Hood References Clear

Cobra measures hood displacement from DOWN, from 0 to 22 degrees. A displacement drawing uses that
reference. A projectile-direction drawing adds 28 degrees once to obtain launch elevation: DOWN is
28, MID is 45, and UP is 50 degrees above horizontal. Label the quantity you show. The Week 6 hood
mesh remains static; the animated component in this exercise is the intake.

## Import the Week 6 Asset

Open `Robot_Cobra2026Week6/README.md` in the asset folder the mentor supplies. It includes the
CAD source, filenames, coordinate conversion, renderer, and reproduction exercise. Keep the
stationary body, intake, and hood separate as `model.glb`, `model_0.glb`, and `model_1.glb`.

Work on a copy. Export the intake separately from the stationary body, preserving its assembly
coordinates. Record units and the source document/version. Convert to GLB if needed, check a known
dimension, and import the copies with the matching configuration. Explain any change needed because
your export has a different origin. Do this before reducing mesh density.

The intake hinge is fixed at robot coordinates (-0.1778, 0, 0.2198) meters in the supplied asset.
Its zeroing transform moves the exported hinge to the component origin. `IntakeVisualization` places
that origin back at the robot hinge and applies measured rotation around Y. The component-pose array
is ordered `[intake, hood]`; changing this order changes which mesh moves.

## Check Three Positions

Render and inspect intake angles of 0, 20, and 90 degrees using the included renderer. Run
`IntakeVisualizationTest` to check the fixed hinge and upward rotation. The intake should rise while
the hood and stationary robot stay in place. A model swinging around the robot center has the wrong
component origin. A model that is 1,000 times too large has the wrong units.

In AdvantageScope, load the project custom assets folder. Add the estimated robot pose to a 3D
Field view, select Cobra 2026 Week 6, and attach `Intake/ComponentPoses` as its component array.
The log tree may place outputs under `RealOutputs`. Compare the animation with measured intake
angle while a goal changes. The model must follow the measurement.

## Show the Result

Save your export choices, asset configuration, three views, and a robot log. Explain the measurement
reference, component order, hinge, and origin conversion. Ask another student to load the result
using your notes and make one requested change. Treat physical clearances and encoder calibration
as separate checks. A correct rendering alone does not establish either.
