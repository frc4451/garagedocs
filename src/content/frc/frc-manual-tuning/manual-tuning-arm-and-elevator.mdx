The [turret recipe](/frc/manual-tuning-turret) with a gravity step in front, and the gravity step
has a trick in it that also finds `kS`. This is the procedure for the mechanisms that fall when you
let go: arms, pivots, hoods, elevators, climbers.

The arm procedure is the turret procedure with a gravity step in front, and the gravity step has a
trick in it that also finds `kS`. The example: a 35:1 arm in `PositionTorqueCurrentFOC` with
`GravityType = Arm_Cosine`.

<StepsBox title="Arm (or elevator) procedure">

1. **Zero all gains.**
2. **Find the smallest `kG` at which the arm stops falling.** Raise it until the arm holds; nudge
   down until it just falls again. This is the *lower* bound: it equals gravity minus friction,
   `kG − kS`, because friction is helping hold it.
3. **Find the largest `kG` at which the arm does not rise.** Keep raising past the hold point until
   the arm starts to climb; nudge down until it just stops. This is the *upper* bound: gravity plus
   friction, `kG + kS`.
4. **`kG` is the midpoint** of the two: `((kG + kS) + (kG − kS)) ÷ 2`.
5. **`kS` is half the difference**: `((kG + kS) − (kG − kS)) ÷ 2`. One bracketing experiment has
   measured both gravity and friction.
6. **Set a nearby setpoint** (0.1 rotations).
7. **Raise `kP` until significant overshoot.**
8. **Raise `kD` until it stops.**
9. **Repeat 7–8** to the latency limit, exactly as for the [turret](/frc/manual-tuning-turret), then
   back off `kD` and `kP` in that order.
10. **Verify at other setpoints**, including the other direction.

</StepsBox>

For an **elevator** the same bracket applies with `GravityType = Elevator_Static`, and it is even
easier to read: the carriage sits still between the two bounds. Do the bracket at mid-travel. For an
arm, do it **horizontal**, where `cos θ = 1` and the gravity term is at its maximum
([Feedforward](/frc/feedforward#the-three-equations)).

**How the worked example went.** `kG` 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 20 (holds), 18, 16 (falls), 17 (falls
appreciably), 17.5 (holds) → lower bound 17.5. Up again: 20, 22, 24, 26 (climbs), 25 (climbs), 24.5
(holds) → upper bound 24.5. So `kG = (24.5 + 17.5) ÷ 2 = 21 A` and `kS = (24.5 − 17.5) ÷ 2 = 3.5 A`.
Then the `kP`/`kD` ladder: `kP` 1 → 16 (overshoot), `kD` 1 → 8; `kP` 32, 64, `kD` 16, 32; `kP` 128,
250, 500, `kD` 64, 128; `kP` 1000, 2000 (a little oscillation), `kD` 250; `kP` 4000, 8000, `kD` 500,
which produced on-the-way oscillation: the limit. `kD` back to 400, 300. A 0.6-rotation setpoint
overshot and `kD` could not rise, so `kP` 4000, 3700. Checked at −0.1 and 0.4. **Final: `kG = 21 A`,
`kS = 3.5 A`, `kP = 3700 A/rot`, `kD = 300 A/rps`.**

This is the procedure behind [PID Tuning Practice – Elevator](/frc/pid-tuning-practice-elevator) and
[– Arm](/frc/pid-tuning-practice-arm) (voltage mode, so volts throughout and much smaller `kP`).
[Elevators](/frc/elevator-example) and [Pivots](/frc/pivot-example) show where those gains and
gravity models belong in reusable components.

## Tuning a Nova Live

The bracket and the ladder change a gain dozens of times. Redeploying for each change is slow, so
make the gains tunable and edit them from the dashboard;
[Tunable Constants and Characterization](/frc/tunable-constants-and-characterization#the-tuning-loop)
shows that loop. On a Thrifty Nova, the gains this procedure tunes are configuration options, and
the profile gains apply while the Nova runs its onboard motion profile:

<ContentTable
  headers={["Procedure step", "Nova option"]}
  rows={[
    ["Gravity bracket", "<code>NovaConfig.Mp0.kG</code> and <code>NovaConfig.Mp0.kS</code>"],
    ["<code>kP</code>/<code>kD</code> ladder", "<code>NovaConfig.Pid0.p</code> and <code>NovaConfig.Pid0.d</code>"],
    ["Gravity model", "<code>NovaConfig.Mp0.gravType(...)</code>: <code>MPGravityType.COSINE</code> for an arm, <code>MPGravityType.CONSTANT</code> for an elevator"]
  ]}
/>

Four things differ from the Talon in CTRE's worked example:

- **Volts, not amps.** The Nova has no torque-current mode, and ThriftyLib documents its profile
  `kS` and `kG` in volts. The example's numbers do not transfer; the procedure does. Expect a much
  smaller `kP`, as in the voltage-mode practice projects.
- **Send only what changed, and only when it changes.** Each `configure(...)` call is one CAN
  transaction, and ThriftyLib warns against large reconfigurations inside the periodic loop. When a
  tunable changes, send only the gain options in one `configure(...)` call, without a factory reset.
- **The Nova remembers.** Its flash settings persist across power cycles. Put
  `CANDeviceConfig.factoryReset()` first in the startup batch, so the next boot starts from the
  constants in code rather than the last value tried on the dashboard. Copy the final values into
  those constants before you finish.
- **Tune on the robot.** ThriftyLib's simulator ignores all PID values, so the `kP`/`kD` ladder does
  nothing in simulation. Bracket and ladder on the physical mechanism.

<RulesBox title="Common mistakes">

- **Bracketing an arm somewhere other than horizontal.** The gravity term is largest at horizontal;
  that is where the bracket measures it.
- **Taking the first hold point as <code>kG</code>.** That is <code>kG − kS</code>. Find the upper
  bound too and take the midpoint.
- **Forgetting the other direction.** Verify moves both up and down, and a far setpoint as well as a
  near one.
- **Accepting overshoot on an arm with hard stops.** Trade a little overdamping on small moves for
  no overshoot on large ones, as the example does.

</RulesBox>
