The second recipe is a position loop, and it introduces the `kP`/`kD` ladder that every position mechanism (turret, arm, elevator, profiled or not) climbs. Learn it here, where gravity is not in the way. The skeleton and the tools are in [Every Gain Is Canonical](/frc/manual-pid-tuning).

Any position mechanism that gravity does not act on (a turret, a horizontal slide, a swerve steer) tunes the same way. The example: a 20:1 turret in `PositionTorqueCurrentFOC`.

One thing changes from voltage mode, and it changes everything about `kD`: **in torque-current control there is no natural damping.** In voltage mode the motor's back-EMF acts as a brake as it speeds up, so a modest `kP` alone often settles without overshoot. In torque mode you are commanding force directly; nothing slows the mechanism down but you. `kD` is not optional.

<StepsBox title="Turret procedure">

1. **Zero all gains.**
2. **Set a nearby setpoint**, about 0.1 mechanism rotations away. A small move keeps the first responses small.
3. **Raise `kS` until the turret just starts to move, then back off to just before it does.** The largest static output that does not move the mechanism is the best friction cancellation.
4. **Raise `kP` until you see significant overshoot.**
5. **Raise `kD` until the overshoot stops.**
6. **Repeat 4 and 5** (more `kP`, then enough `kD` to tame it) until one of two things happens: raising `kD` adds oscillation, or the mechanism oscillates *on its way to* the setpoint (not just around it). Either means you have hit the latency limit. Lower `kD` until the on-the-way oscillation stops, then lower `kP` until any remaining overshoot stops. The goal is the highest `kP` and `kD` the system will tolerate, because that is the shortest travel time.
7. **Verify at other setpoints** and tune `kP`/`kD` for the cases the mechanism actually sees.

</StepsBox>

**How the worked example went.** `kS`: 1 did not move, 2 did, back to 1.5, 1.25 (still), 1.3 moved → `kS = 1.25`. `kP = 1`, then 10 with heavy overshoot; `kD = 1`, then 10, which is overdamped and fine because `kP` goes up next: 20, 50, 100 (slight overshoot), 200 (significant) → `kD` 20, then 40 (overdamped again) → `kP` 500, 1000, 2000 (overshoot) → `kD` 60 → `kP` 3000 oscillates → `kD` 80, 100 always oscillating: **the limit.** Back down: `kD` 80 (oscillates on the way), 70 (clean); `kP` 2800 overdamped, `kD` 65; `kP` 2600, 2500, 2400, which looked good. Then a 0.45-rotation setpoint overshot badly, and `kD` could not go higher without on-the-way oscillation at small moves, so `kP` came down: 2200, 2000, 1800 (overdamped), `kD` 60, `kP` 1700, 1650. **Final: `kS = 1.25 A`, `kP = 1650 A/rot`, `kD = 60 A/rps`**, with a little overdamping at 0.1 rotations accepted as the price of not overshooting at 0.45.

Two lessons in that example. The values are large. Thousands of amps per rotation is normal in torque-current position control, because the error is in mechanism rotations and a tenth of a rotation must produce a real force; do not be alarmed. And the ending is a **trade-off**, which brings up the last section of this page.

### Near and far: when one set of gains is not enough

Both position examples ended the same way: the gains that were ideal for a 0.1-rotation move overshot a bigger one, and the compromise was a slightly overdamped small move. That compromise is usually fine. When it is not, for a mechanism that needs crisp small corrections and long travels, the answer is **gain scheduling** on the size of the error: Slot 0 tuned for the within-one-rotation case, Slot 1 for the far case, and the control request's `withSlot(…)` chosen from the current error. Phoenix has three slots for this reason. Tune each slot with the procedure above at *its* representative setpoints; do not try to find one set of numbers that pretends the two cases are the same.

<RulesBox title="Common mistakes">

- **Skipping <code>kD</code> in torque-current mode.** Nothing else slows the mechanism down.
- **Chasing on-the-way oscillation with more <code>kD</code>.** That oscillation <em>is</em> too much <code>kD</code>. Back off.
- **Tuning only the 0.1-rotation move.** Check a far setpoint before calling it done, and decide which case the mechanism actually lives in.
- **Being alarmed by the size of the numbers.** Thousands of amps per mechanism rotation is normal here.

</RulesBox>
