### The assumption this section makes

From here on, the FRC track assumes two things: you have **completed [Kit Bot](/kit-bot)** (the 2026 KitBot rebuilt end to end and demonstrated in simulation) and you have **mastery of Java** as taught in [Java Training](/java) and proven in the [Practice Assignments](/assignments). Lessons will say "you did this in R07" and link to it rather than re-teaching it. If a link takes you somewhere unfamiliar, that is the page to do first.

Two groups are deliberately shared with Kit Bot, [Environment Setup](/frc/environment-setup) and [Basics](/frc/intro-to-motors), because a returning student may need to reinstall or re-read one API without redoing the whole build. Everything else in FRC is new material.

### What changes

Kit Bot taught you the *pattern*: an IO interface, real and sim implementations, a subsystem that cannot tell which it has, telemetry to AdvantageScope, a runtime selector. Cobra is that pattern at competition scale. Most of the differences are things FRC 4451 stopped writing by hand:

### How to use the rest of this section

Read [Reading Our Robot Code](/frc/team-robot-code) next. It is a guided tour of the repository, with the map above in hand. Then read [Lombok](/frc/lombok), which appears on half the classes the tour shows. Three more things the tour points at that Kit Bot never had, [VirtualSubsystem](/frc/virtual-subsystem), [BobotState](/frc/bobot-state) and the two-part [Math Toolbox](/frc/math-toolbox), have their own group, **Cobra Architecture**, near the end, because they only make sense once pose estimation and shooting exist. Between here and there the groups follow the order FRC 4451 builds a robot in: the command-based structure Cobra uses with AdvantageKit and the IO layer, simulation (before anything is tuned, including unit tests and replay), control theory and tuning, the Phoenix side of motor control, mechanism examples read from Cobra, pose estimation and vision, and finally autonomous.
