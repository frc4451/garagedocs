### The assumption this section makes

From here on, the FRC track assumes two things: you have **completed [Kit Bot](/kit-bot)** (the 2026 KitBot rebuilt end to end and demonstrated in simulation) and you have **mastery of Java** as taught in [Java Training](/java) and proven in the [Practice Assignments](/java/assignments). Lessons will say "you did this in R07" and link to it rather than re-teaching it. If a link takes you somewhere unfamiliar, that is the page to do first.

Two groups are deliberately shared with Kit Bot, [Environment Setup](/frc/environment-setup) and [Basics](/frc/introduction-to-motors), because a returning student may need to reinstall or re-read one API without redoing the whole build. Everything else in FRC is new material.

### How to use the rest of this section

Read [Cobra Repository Tour](/frc/cobra-repository-tour) next. It is a guided tour of the repository, with the map above in hand. Then read [Lombok](/frc/lombok), which appears on half the classes the tour shows. Three more things the tour points at that Kit Bot never had, [VirtualSubsystem](/frc/virtual-subsystem), [BobotState](/frc/bobot-state) and the two-part [Math Toolbox](/frc/math-toolbox-geometry), have their own group, **Cobra Architecture**, near the end, because they only make sense once pose estimation and shooting exist. Between here and there, the curriculum follows the dependencies in the code: command-based structure; IO, simulation, and debugging; control theory and reusable mechanisms; pose estimation and vision; Cobra's shared robot state; autonomous; and finally robot-wide state coordination.
