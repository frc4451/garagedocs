### Use the regulator selected for the coprocessor

FRC 4451 uses two fixed-output Pololu step-up/step-down regulators for PhotonVision coprocessors:

| Coprocessor | Team regulator | Regulated output | Published input range | Published current description |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Orange Pi 5 | [S13V30F5, item 4082](https://www.pololu.com/product/4082) | `5 V` | `2.8 V` to `22 V` | About `3 A` typical near the output voltage; actual continuous current depends on input voltage and heat |
| Rubik Pi 3 | [S13V25F12, item 4984](https://www.pololu.com/product/4984) | `12 V` | `2.8 V` to `22 V` | About `2.5 A` typical near the output voltage; actual continuous current depends on input voltage and heat |

Both regulators can increase or decrease voltage. This matters on a robot because battery voltage can be above or below the desired output during use. The regulator keeps the coprocessor input near its fixed output voltage while the battery changes, provided the regulator remains within its input, current, and thermal limits.

The current printed in a product name is not a guarantee for every installation. Check Pololu's continuous-output graph at the expected robot input voltage. Include the coprocessor, cameras, USB devices, and startup current in the load estimate.

### Orange Pi 5: regulated 5 V

Use the **S13V30F5** for the Orange Pi 5 power circuit. It produces a fixed `5 V` output. Pololu specifies `5 V` with 3% accuracy and protection for input undervoltage, output overvoltage, overcurrent, reverse voltage, and overtemperature.

```text
protected robot branch -> VIN   S13V30F5   VOUT -> Orange Pi 5 V input
robot ground           -> GND                GND -> Orange Pi ground
```

Do not connect raw robot battery voltage to the Orange Pi's 5 V input. Confirm `5 V` and polarity with a meter before plugging in the board.

### Rubik Pi 3: regulated 12 V

Use the **S13V25F12** for the Rubik Pi 3 power circuit. It produces a fixed `12 V` output. Its step-up/step-down design can hold the output near 12 V when robot battery voltage moves above or below 12 V, within the regulator's operating limits.

<ReferenceImage

```text
protected robot branch -> VIN   S13V25F12   VOUT -> Rubik Pi 3 12 V input
robot ground           -> GND                 GND -> Rubik Pi ground
```

Do not power the Rubik Pi 3 from the Orange Pi's 5 V regulator. Do not treat raw battery voltage as a steady 12 V supply.

### Build and test the power circuit

1. Verify the regulator's item number and output marking before soldering.
2. Connect the protected robot branch to `VIN` and `GND`.
3. Connect `VOUT` and `GND` to the coprocessor using the correct connector polarity.
4. Insulate the regulator from the robot frame and conductive debris.
5. Add strain relief and leave room for airflow around the regulator.
6. Power the regulator without the coprocessor and measure the output voltage.
7. Turn power off, connect the coprocessor, and repeat the measurement under load.
8. Test while cameras and other USB devices are active.
9. Watch for resets during robot startup and drivetrain acceleration.
10. Check regulator temperature after a realistic test period. Pololu warns that the board can become hot enough to burn skin.

<RulesBox title="Done when">

The electrical drawing names Pololu item 4082 for the Orange Pi 5 or item 4984 for the Rubik Pi 3. It also records the protected input branch, connector polarity, expected load, and wire path. The measured output remains close to 5 V or 12 V under a realistic coprocessor and camera load without resets or unsafe heating.

</RulesBox>
