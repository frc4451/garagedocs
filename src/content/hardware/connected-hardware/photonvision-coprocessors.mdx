### Choose the board before building its power circuit

GarageDocs covers two PhotonVision coprocessors used by the team:

| Coprocessor | Required input for this curriculum | Team regulator | Key warning |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Orange Pi 5 family | **5 V regulated** | [Pololu S13V30F5, item 4082](https://www.pololu.com/product/4082) | Never connect raw robot battery voltage to the 5 V input |
| Rubik Pi 3 | **12 V regulated** | [Pololu S13V25F12, item 4984](https://www.pololu.com/product/4984) | Do not use the Orange Pi's 5 V supply or assume raw battery voltage is a steady 12 V |

These are step-up/step-down regulators. They can correct an input voltage that is above or below their fixed output, within their operating limits. This helps the coprocessor see a steady supply while robot battery voltage changes.

The regulator still needs enough continuous output for the coprocessor, cameras, attached USB devices, and startup demand. Pololu's achievable output current depends on input voltage and heat. Check the product graph instead of using the headline current alone.

### Wire and verify power first

1. Confirm the coprocessor model and the regulator item number.
2. Connect the regulator input to an appropriate protected robot-power branch.
3. Check `VIN`, `VOUT`, ground, and connector polarity against both manuals.
4. Insulate and secure the regulator, then add strain relief to both sides.
5. With the coprocessor disconnected, power the regulator and measure its output.
6. Turn power off before connecting the coprocessor.
7. Power the complete system and measure the voltage under load.
8. Repeat the test with every camera and USB device active.
9. Watch for coprocessor restarts during robot startup and drivetrain acceleration.
10. Check regulator temperature after sustained use.

The expected result is a stable `5 V` supply at the Orange Pi or a stable `12 V` supply at the Rubik Pi, with no restart when the robot voltage changes during a realistic test.

### Install PhotonVision

1. Download the release for the exact board model.
2. Verify the filename before writing an image or installing a package.
3. Complete first boot on the bench with the verified regulated supply.
4. Connect Ethernet and open the PhotonVision web interface.
5. Set the team number, hostname, and network mode.
6. Confirm every attached camera is matched to the intended configuration.
7. Export a settings backup after networking, cameras, pipelines, and calibration are complete.

### Use the PhotonVision web client

The web interface is the hardware client for the coprocessor. Use it to inspect cameras, select resolutions, tune pipelines, calibrate each camera at its operating resolution, update PhotonVision, read logs, and export configuration.

Camera calibration belongs to the physical camera and resolution. Replacing a camera or changing resolution requires a new calibration. Keep the mount rigid, protect connectors from vibration, and secure Ethernet and camera cables with strain relief.

### Verify before robot code

- The measured supply stays near 5 V for Orange Pi 5 or 12 V for Rubik Pi 3 under load.
- The coprocessor stays powered through robot startup and a drivetrain acceleration test.
- The PhotonVision page remains reachable at the documented hostname or address.
- Each expected camera produces a live image.
- The network settings show the correct team.
- A saved settings archive can restore the device.

Then continue to [PhotonVision](/frc/photonvision) for pipelines and robot-code integration.
