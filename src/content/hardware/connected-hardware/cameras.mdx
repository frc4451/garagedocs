### Choose a camera for the job

A camera choice affects image quality, motion blur, field of view, USB bandwidth, mounting, and calibration. Start with the task the camera must perform.

| Camera or sensor | Image | Published maximum at full resolution | USB connection | Useful starting point |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Arducam OV9281 model | Black and white, global shutter | Check the exact camera SKU | Depends on the exact Arducam product | AprilTag tracking |
| Arducam OV9782 model | Color, global shutter | Check the exact camera SKU | Depends on the exact Arducam product | Object or game-piece detection that needs color |

**UVC** means USB Video Class. A UVC camera can use standard video support instead of a custom camera driver. Confirm that the operating system and PhotonVision detect the exact camera before building the final mount.

Global shutter captures the whole image at one moment. This reduces the shape distortion that a rolling shutter can produce while the robot or target moves. Exposure time can still create blur, so lighting and camera settings remain important.

### Team code shows models, not the complete physical installation

Cobra's vision code contains OV9281 and OV9782 simulation presets. Its active intake object-detection configuration selects an OV9782 model. These constants document sensor assumptions and simulated field of view. They do not prove the exact product SKU, lens, cable, mount, or camera installed at an event.

Read the physical label and record these details:

- manufacturer and product SKU
- image sensor and color or black-and-white output
- lens and field of view
- selected resolution and frame rate
- USB version, cable, and coprocessor port
- mount location and orientation
- device name or serial number
- PhotonVision camera configuration and calibration date

### Arducam options

Arducam sells several UVC cameras built around OV9281 and OV9782 sensors. Identify the exact product before selecting a setup tool or copying specifications. Cameras with the same sensor can use different lenses, USB interfaces, enclosures, and supported identity tools.

Arducam documents two configuration tools for supported cameras:

- **UVC Config Tool:** reads firmware and can edit the device name or serial number on selected USB 3 cameras.
- **Serial Number Tool:** edits the device name and serial number on selected USB 2 cameras.

Choose the tool from the exact camera interface and supported-device list.

### Prepare any camera for PhotonVision

1. Record the exact model, sensor, lens, interface, cable, coprocessor port, and mount location.
2. Connect one camera and confirm that the operating system and PhotonVision detect it.
3. Assign a stable name or unique serial number when the manufacturer supports it.
4. Reconnect the camera and confirm that its identity persists.
5. Select a resolution and frame rate the camera, USB connection, and coprocessor can sustain.
6. Set exposure and gain while viewing the real target under expected lighting.
7. Focus the lens and lock it mechanically when the hardware supports that step.
8. Calibrate that physical camera at its operating resolution.
9. Reboot the coprocessor and confirm PhotonVision restores the correct camera configuration.
10. Export the PhotonVision settings.

With several identical cameras, stable device identity helps PhotonVision restore the correct configuration after a reboot or USB-port change. A rigid mount and locking cable reduce movement and disconnects, but they do not replace strain relief.

The hardware record identifies the product SKU, sensor, lens, USB connection, mount, resolution, frame rate, and stable device identity. PhotonVision restores the correct configuration after a reboot. The saved calibration belongs to the installed camera at its operating resolution.
