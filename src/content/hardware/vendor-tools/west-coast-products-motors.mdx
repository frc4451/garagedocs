### Separate the motor from its controller

West Coast Products (WCP) supplies the motor assemblies. CTRE supplies the controller design and Phoenix software used with them. Name both parts when they are separate.

| WCP motor | Controller | Team evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Kraken X60 | Integrated Talon FX | Cobra drivetrain and shooter configuration |
| Kraken X44 | Integrated Talon FX | Cobra shared motor catalog; confirm a physical installation in the robot hardware record |
| Minion | Separate Talon FXS or another documented compatible controller | Cobra hood model and reusable Talon FXS code |

The Kraken X60 and X44 combine a motor and Talon FX controller in one assembly. The Minion is a separate motor. A controller such as the Talon FXS must be configured for it.

### Choose the motor for the mechanism

Compare these properties before choosing or replacing a motor:

- physical size, mounting pattern, shaft, and connector access
- free speed, torque, current, and thermal limits
- integrated or separate controller
- gearbox ratio and available pinions
- required sensor connection
- current limit and breaker plan
- space for wires, covers, and service tools

The X60 and X44 are not interchangeable in code or hardware merely because both use `TalonFX`. The correct motor model affects simulation, current limits, expected speed, mechanism gearing, and replacement parts.

### Record and verify the installation

1. Read the motor and controller labels.
2. Record the motor model, controller model, CAN ID, bus, mechanism, and electrical branch.
3. Inspect the shaft, mounting, terminals, and phase or sensor wiring against the current manual.
4. Use Phoenix Tuner X to identify the controller.
5. Verify the motor arrangement when the motor and controller are separate.
6. Compare the current limit, mechanism ratio, and `DCMotor` model with the physical assembly.
7. Test at low voltage and watch direction, current, velocity, and temperature.

A `DCMotor` name tells simulation which mathematical model to use. It does not configure real hardware or prove that the modeled motor was installed.

<RulesBox title="Done when">

The hardware record names the WCP motor and CTRE controller separately where needed. Phoenix Tuner X, physical labels, mechanism constants, and simulation model all describe the same installed hardware.
