### Choose between the two cameras

The ThriftyCam and Thriftiest Cam are black-and-white global-shutter USB cameras sold for robot vision. Their names are similar, but their sensors, interfaces, maximum resolutions, frame rates, cases, and weights differ.

| Camera | Published full-resolution maximum | USB connection | Case and weight | Useful starting point |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| ThriftyCam, `TTB-0241` | `1600 × 1304` at `60 FPS` | USB 3.0 UVC with locking USB-C cable | Machined aluminum, `0.128 lb` | Higher-resolution AprilTag images when the coprocessor has USB 3 bandwidth |
| Thriftiest Cam, `TTB-0370` | `1280 × 800` at `120 FPS` | USB 2.0 with locking cable | Molded case, `0.06 lb` | A smaller, lighter camera or a USB 2 connection |

**UVC** means USB Video Class. A UVC camera uses standard video support instead of a custom camera driver. Confirm that the operating system and PhotonVision detect the exact camera before building the final mount.

Global shutter captures the whole image at one moment. This reduces the shape distortion that a rolling shutter can produce while the robot or target moves. Exposure time can still create blur.

### ThriftyCam

The ThriftyCam uses an OMNIVISION OG02B1B black-and-white global-shutter sensor. The current product page specifies `1600 × 1304` at up to `60 FPS` over USB 3.0. It includes an aluminum case, an 80-degree M12 lens, and a locking USB-C cable.

Use a USB 3 port when you need the published maximum performance. Sharing a USB controller with several high-bandwidth cameras can still limit the available resolution or frame rate. Confirm the achieved settings in PhotonVision.

### Thriftiest Cam

The Thriftiest Cam uses an OMNIVISION OV9281 black-and-white global-shutter sensor. The product page specifies `1280 × 800` at up to `120 FPS`. It uses USB 2.0 and includes a locking cable and molded case.

Its lighter weight and USB 2 connection can help when maximum ThriftyCam resolution is unnecessary. The higher published frame rate does not guarantee a better vision pipeline. Exposure, lighting, lens, processing load, and network timing also affect the result.

### Record the complete installation

The camera model alone does not describe the vision system. Record:

- product SKU and sensor
- lens and field of view
- selected resolution and frame rate
- USB version, cable, and coprocessor port
- mount location and orientation
- PhotonVision camera name and calibration date

Cobra's vision code contains a ThriftyCam simulation preset. That preset documents assumptions used by simulation. It does not prove which camera, lens, cable, or mount was installed on the physical robot at an event.

### Bring up the camera

1. Connect one camera to the intended coprocessor port.
2. Confirm that the operating system and PhotonVision detect it.
3. Give it a stable, descriptive name.
4. Select a resolution and frame rate the camera, USB connection, and coprocessor can sustain.
5. Set exposure and gain while viewing the real target under expected lighting.
6. Focus the lens and lock it mechanically when the hardware supports that step.
7. Calibrate the installed camera at its operating resolution.
8. Add strain relief without forcing the connector sideways.
9. Reboot the coprocessor and confirm PhotonVision restores the correct configuration.
10. Export the PhotonVision settings.

The hardware record names the exact Thrifty Bot camera, lens, USB port, mount, resolution, frame rate, and PhotonVision configuration. The cable is locked and strain-relieved. PhotonVision restores the correct camera and calibration after a reboot.
