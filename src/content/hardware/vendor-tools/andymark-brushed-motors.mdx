### A brushed motor is not its controller

The CIM and AndyMark RedLine are brushed DC motors. They need a compatible motor controller, such as a SPARK MAX configured for brushed operation or another legal controller that supports the motor. The motor does not contain a Talon FX or an integrated NEO-style hall sensor.

| Motor family | Useful characteristic | Main beginner concern |
|---|---|---|
| CIM | Larger, heavier FRC motor with a keyed shaft | High stall current and no integrated position sensor |
| RedLine or another 775-class motor | Smaller, high-speed motor | Heat builds quickly when stalled or heavily loaded |

Scorpion includes a SPARK MAX brushed-motor implementation named for CIMs. Finn also uses a SPARK MAX in brushed mode. The Java motor type confirms brushed control, but the physical label confirms the motor model.

### Commission a brushed mechanism

1. Read the motor label and product number.
2. Confirm the motor is legal under the current FRC rules.
3. Choose a controller that supports brushed operation.
4. Set the controller to brushed mode before connecting or running the motor.
5. Select the breaker, wire size, and current limit for the controller, motor, and mechanism.
6. Add an encoder or limit switch when the mechanism needs measured position or speed.
7. Test at low voltage while watching current and temperature.
8. Stop immediately if the mechanism stalls, binds, or heats rapidly.

Do not use a brushless motor model or expect an integrated encoder reading from a bare CIM or RedLine. The feedback device must exist on the mechanism and in the code.

<RulesBox title="Done when">

The hardware record names the motor, controller, control mode, breaker, current limit, and feedback device. A low-voltage test confirms direction and shows no binding or unexpected heating.

</RulesBox>
